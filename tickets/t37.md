# T37: Trustworthy Research Memory, Evidence Selection, and Continuity

**Owner:** Blue. **Status:** Open for review in [PR #45](https://github.com/bkramerext/sigma/pull/45). **Priority:** High, non-cloud alpha feature and reliability work.

## Why this matters

SIGMA should help a human make genuine discoveries by building reliably on what was learned, what failed, and what still needs testing. A growing research history must **not** cause agents to forget a decisive objection, revive a refuted claim, repeat expensive dead ends, or stop working because irrelevant old artifacts filled the prompt. The user must be able to trust that every assertion of progress is grounded in inspectable evidence.

T17.8's deterministic quality tests are encouraging but do **not** demonstrate live-model discovery or reliable mathematical judgment. This ticket strengthens the underlying research substrate; do not describe it as having established model quality.

## Current system and gap to investigate first

Read `docs/BRAINSTORM.md` ("Context management and retrieval"), `tickets/t17.8.md`, `docs/PROJECT.md`, T27/T28/T29 changes, and the current `src/sigma/research_sessions.py` and research tests.

The code **already** builds deterministic role contexts, includes prior-session findings and failures, carries outstanding obligations, and fails closed when `MAX_AGENT_CONTEXT_BYTES` is exceeded. Respect and preserve these strengths.

But context enters through several paths: `_agent_context`, `_portfolio_context`, prior-session indices, peer handoffs, computation citations, and human interventions. Prior-session findings are bounded in recency/order and bytes, while in-session full artifacts accumulate and can hit a hard context limit. Investigate whether important contradictory or unresolved evidence can be crowded out, repeated, inconsistently attributed, or made hard for the user to inspect.

**First deliverable:** a short evidence-based failure/risk inventory with reproducible deterministic scenarios before major refactoring. If a suspected defect is not reproducible, record that and do not invent it.

## User outcomes

1. **Dependable research memory:** Each Investigator and Skeptic sees the important, applicable prior work needed to advance its *narrow, testable strategy question*, including unresolved objections and failed attempts. A failed session does not erase or invalidate its durable strategy.
2. **Productive next steps:** The agent can identify what has actually changed since the previous attempt, why an avenue is still promising, and a concrete discriminating next test. Where evidence is insufficient, say so rather than fabricate an answer.
3. **No false confidence:** Provenance and epistemic status survive cross-session reuse, cross-strategy transfer, computation, human guidance, and resume. A cited model claim, finite test, or peer agreement is not independent verification.
4. **Explainability:** A human can inspect what evidence an agent was given, which important evidence was withheld and why, and what the agent did with it, without wading through raw prompts by default.
5. **Reliability:** Work remains bounded, restartable, predictable in cost/calls, and recoverable. Failure is explicit, understandable, and does not erase or silently change research history.

## Scope: one coherent substantial implementation

### A. Evidence/obligation model and policy

- Derive a canonical **research-context assembly policy** (module or bounded helpers, not a new general agent framework) for legacy-compatible single-strategy and current multi-strategy sessions, both roles, later sessions, and resumed sessions.
- Prioritize relevant *currently unresolved* critical objections and proof obligations, current strategy question, current Investigator output for Skeptic, applicable human guidance, and material counterexamples before optional historical prose. Distinguish unresolved, addressed, superseded, retracted, and unknown; never infer resolution merely from absence in a newer summary.
- Preserve exact source identifiers (project/session/strategy/artifact/step/execution where available), status, and assumption/domain scope. Explicitly distinguish an algorithmically selected summary from an original source artifact.
- Deterministic selection, stable ordering, deduplication, bounded text and count, and an explicit context-byte budget. A byte budget is a safety ceiling, **not** a promised model-token estimate.
- Include meaningful prior unsuccessful attempts and reasons not to repeat them. Avoid spurious transfer when assumptions/domains differ or evidence was retracted. Do not label a research strategy itself failed merely because a run failed.
- Treat all stored project/research text and previous model output as **untrusted data** when constructing prompts; it cannot change SIGMA's roles, tools, budget, billing, or verification policy.

### B. Space pressure without silent evidence loss

- When the full history will not fit, compact or omit lower-priority evidence **with a recorded reason**, retaining references back to the original durable records. Use structured, deterministic extracts before creating any AI-generated summaries.
- **Never silently discard a decisive counterexample, active human instruction, material unresolved objection, or current role's required input.** If even mandatory evidence does not fit, stop before dispatch with a clear, recoverable user-facing reason and a precise audit note. Do not truncate it to appear resolved.
- Distinguish omitted optional details from unreviewed critical evidence. Maintain a meaningful bounded omission count and source pointers.
- Keep the input usable at increasing history sizes (e.g. 1, 10, 100+ artifacts); don't create a search index or vector DB for the alpha unless measurements show a concrete need and the coordinator authorizes a separate scope change.

### C. Traceable research progress and human inspection

- Persist a compact **context manifest per dispatched agent step**, linked to the original step, with selected source IDs, relevance/priority rationale or category, omitted counts/reasons, policy version and budget decisions. Protect saved history and export compatibility; inspect the existing artifact and event model before adding new schema.
- Make this information discoverable through the existing session/step evidence or audit UI with minimal, consistent copper/dark styling. Prefer an understated "Context used" disclosure over a new panel or large redesign. If the existing UI can adequately show the manifest, reuse it.
- Retain and correctly represent human intervention, computation provenance, peer handoff reviews, retractions, and explicit model acknowledgment. Do **not** mistake model self-reported uptake for proof of correct use.
- Make any new "next test"/progress explanation grounded in the saved agent output and evidence. Do not generate synthetic discovery claims or require an additional provider call to produce a progress display.

### D. Failure isolation, recovery, and correctness

- Ensure context selection is stable under pause/resume, process restart, and stale/in-flight calls. No silent re-query of mutable historical context midway through a dispatched step, no repeated billing, and no retroactive modification of evidence or human guidance.
- Exercise the shared concurrency path: two strategies with conflicting peer evidence, interventions targeting one role/branch, interruption near delivery, and one branch/provider failure while another proceeds.
- Preserve existing call, time, output, and estimated-cost guards and safe behavior for unknown prices or transport failures. No automatic retries or billing fallback.
- If the new context manifest cannot be saved, fail safely before provider dispatch. Existing persisted sessions remain readable.

## Required acceptance tests and examples

Implement deterministic tests (not merely prompt-string assertions) covering:

1. A *false but plausible* earlier finding contradicted by a saved concrete counterexample: context conveys the contradiction and final outcome never promotes the false claim as verified.
2. A long 100+ artifact history exceeding the previous naive context budget: relevant unresolved obligations, current question, and a recent decisive disproof still reach the intended agent; optional older material is omitted with manifest and no fabricated resolution.
3. An over-budget **mandatory evidence** set: explicit recoverable failure before provider call, no data loss, no hidden charge.
4. Prior failed attempt across sessions followed by a distinct bounded next experiment; original strategy/session provenance remains intact.
5. Cross-strategy evidence that shares vocabulary but not assumptions, plus a retracted finding: no silent cross-domain promotion.
6. Paused/restarted concurrent session with one intervention and interrupted call: deterministic manifest and delivery accounting; no duplicate model/computation execution.
7. Skeptic receives applicable Investigator computation and exact execution/source provenance; invalid/missing citations are not manufactured.
8. Legacy record compatibility, persisted context-manifest reload and export/report behavior.
9. Real Chrome end-to-end fixture journey showing understandable context/progress/audit evidence on desktop and narrow mobile widths; no horizontal overflow and no unrelated UX churn.
10. Reasonable performance and storage at the history bound; demonstrable invariants instead of arbitrary benchmarks.

For each important test, document the baseline risk, observed behavior before/after, and why it protects **research trustworthiness** or human progress. Preserve existing regression coverage; do not alter a fixture to camouflage a product failure.
