Here is the complete argument. Regions mean connected components of the complement of the union of the entire, pairwise distinct lines in the Euclidean plane. For an existing arrangement, its regions are precisely the nonempty fixed-sign sets of affine equations for its lines. Such sets are intersections of open half-planes, hence open and convex. If a new line has t distinct intersection points with the previous lines, those points divide it into t+1 intervals, including the case t=0. Each interval belongs to one old region. Different intervals belong to different regions: otherwise convexity would force a segment containing an old intersection point to lie inside an old region. Conversely, every old region meeting the new line contains one of these intervals. Thus exactly t+1 old regions meet the new line. Each is split into exactly two nonempty convex pieces: openness supplies points on both sides, and intersection with either open half-plane is convex. Other regions are unchanged, and deleting additional points from the complement cannot merge old components. Consequently the increase is exactly t+1. At insertion k there are at most k−1 distinct intersections with earlier lines, so the increase is at most k. Starting with the single region of the empty arrangement gives the universal upper bound 1+Σ(k=1 to n)k = 1+n(n+1)/2. Neither parallel pairs nor multiple concurrence invalidate this bound. For n=0, the complement is the whole plane and has one region, agreeing with the same formula. Hence the greatest possible number is M(n)=1+n(n+1)/2 for every nonnegative integer n.
