## Research-specific standards and what they mean in this case

- **MIT Mathematics for Computer Science** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-1200j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2024/resources/lecture-notes/): proof techniques, induction, recurrence derivation, modular arithmetic; selected lectures are publicly accessible.
- **Stanford proofwriting checklist**, full HTML (https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1262/proofwriting_checklist): explicit hypotheses and target, scoped variables and quantifiers, causal/load-bearing justification of each assertion.
- **Flajolet & Sedgewick, _Analytic Combinatorics_**, accessible author booksite (https://sedgewick.io/books/analytic-combinatorics/): generation/transfer-matrix modeling and distinguishing exact structural identities from asymptotic dominance claims. Chapter V transfer matrices and chapters IV–VI asymptotics; never claim to have read full text solely from web synopsis.
- **Bertsekas, mathematical writing rules** (https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/bertsekas/ten-simple-rules-for-mathematical-writing/): precise notation, cases and counterexamples.
- **Poonen, mathematical writing reference** (https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/writing.pdf): unambiguous quantifiers, distinct justification per implication, smaller lemmas; supplemental freely posted PDF, not blindly repeated text.

**Investigator research requirements:** model exact combinatorial objects and edge cases; derive and check finite identities with exact integer arithmetic or reproducible symbolic computation when available; prove statements symbolically for all asserted indices or mark conjectural; use definitions/lemmas leading to global recurrence proof and separate asymptotic argument; record *why* chosen method is valid and what fails. Make meaningful progress even if no final full proof is reached.

**Skeptic research requirements:** attack false recurrences with explicit counterexample index; challenge extrapolation from finite checks; verify offset and exponent in q-binomial identities; inspect n=0,1,2 initial ranges and phase ordering; check coefficients/quantifiers/ranges of a claimed recurrence; distinguish eigenvalue evidence from a proved strict growth bound; test every step of 'sum residue tends to one third' rather than inferring from numerical convergence. Find a stronger or independent proof where feasible, not just generic doubt.

**Epistemic classes:** exact finite computed count, combinatorial bijection (if proved), formal general recurrence (if proved), claimed candidate recurrence (if not proved), spectral growth inequality (if proved), asymptotic conjecture (if open), and incomplete/failed Skeptic review. Agreement between Investigator/Skeptic does not certify formal verification. A known answer or archived paper is not automatically an independently validated reference.
