# Method sources consulted for Phase 2 iteration 1

Accessed 2026-11-01. These short operational notes are not a substitute for Chat's independent scientific evaluation. Clinical-trial and animal-design guidance is applied only to the relevant research method; no blanket checklist is imposed on mathematics or systems analysis.

| Source and section | Rule used here | Applicability and limit |
|---|---|---|
| National Academies, *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science* (2019), ch. 2, [Scientific Methods and Knowledge](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/5), especially core principles and uncertainty; ch. 3, [Understanding Reproducibility and Replicability](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/6) | Distinguish repeat computation on the same data/code from replication on new data; report confidence and uncertainty; preserve correction and challenge as scientific progress. | Applies across the three topics. Matching project exports and successful sessions do not establish the truth of their claims or generalizability.
| Cochrane Handbook, ch. 4, [Searching for and selecting studies](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04), §§4.3–4.6 | Record searched sources, terms, timing, and exclusions; treat studies rather than multiple reports as the evidence unit; disclose coverage and likely gaps. | Adapted for source searches, especially GEO/publication review. This is not a requirement to perform a clinical systematic review for every domain.
| Cochrane Handbook, ch. 8, [Assessing risk of bias in a randomized trial](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-08), §§8.2–8.7 | Assess a specific asserted result and its design, missingness, measurement, and reporting risks, with reasons. | Trial-specific categories apply only to an actual randomized intervention result. They do not certify theoretical or software arguments.
| NIST/SEMATECH, [Engineering Statistics Handbook §5.3.3](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri33.htm) | Choose comparative, screening, or response-surface methods to match the research objective and factor structure. | Useful for proposed system comparisons and experimental design; no experiment was run in this checkpoint.
| Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, [Abduction](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abduction/), revised 2025 | Separate hypothesis generation from inference to the best explanation; an attractive explanation is not thereby proved. | Applies to candidate agent mechanisms, federation architecture, and biological mechanisms.
| Center for Open Science, [Preregistration](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg) | Label analyses as exploratory or confirmatory; distinguish plans made in advance from explanations fitted after seeing results. | Existing saved data and Phase 1 outputs make this baseline review exploratory; any confirmatory biological analysis needs a prior analysis plan and held-out evidence.
| The Turing Way, [Reproducibility Methods](https://book.the-turing-way.org/project-design/pd-overview/pd-overview-methods/) | Specify the question, collected data, analysis, tools, and infrastructure needed to reproduce a result. | Directly informs the required source manifests and saved execution trace.
| NC3Rs Experimental Design Assistant, [Allocation](https://eda.nc3rs.org.uk/experimental-design-allocation) | Define the experimental unit, randomize allocation, and block relevant nuisance variables; conceal allocation and blind measurements when feasible. | Applies only to a future animal experiment. No animal allocation or intervention is claimed here.

Additional context-specific references named in Issue #432 (NASA trade studies, ACM SIGSOFT empirical standards, NIH rigor, NIST ARIA, Hernán–Robins, and MIT proof methods) were not needed to resolve this input/execution gate and are not claimed as read for this checkpoint. The full sources remain available for Chat's subsequent scientific review.
