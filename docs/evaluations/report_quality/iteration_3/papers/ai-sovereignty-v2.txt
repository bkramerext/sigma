Sovereign AI Federation v2

Abstract
The investigation asks: Can universities with uneven GPU capacity provide useful, reliable AI research access while retaining control of sensitive data and staying within hard budgets? The frozen record includes a source-linked finding that is not independently verified here: With n_G,local,S=200, the expected sensitive gate μ_G,S≥160 requires q_G,local,S≥0.80. The analysis distinguishes recorded arguments from demonstrated results, identifies remaining gaps, and does not claim experiments or proof steps absent from the frozen record.

Problem framing
The investigation asks: Can universities with uneven GPU capacity provide useful, reliable AI research access while retaining control of sensitive data and staying within hard budgets?

Methods and evidence
This synthesis organizes the project's frozen research records and their saved evidence references. It does not claim new experiments.

Background and research context
The central research question is whether universities with uneven GPU capacity can provide useful AI research access while preserving campus authority over data and expenditure; federation is one candidate architecture, not the assumed answer. The relevant comparison therefore concerns authorized, useful research outcomes rather than aggregate GPU capacity or nominal inference throughput. The recorded propositions establish three distinct questions: whether a request may be authorized, whether its financial exposure can remain within hard caps, and whether additional execution destinations improve service enough to justify their costs and obligations. These questions must remain separate because neither authorization safety nor budget preservation establishes task quality, availability, or a federation advantage [C1–C5; session:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc].
MindRouter supplies the intended campus-gateway starting point. The supplied research attributes protocol translation across OpenAI, Ollama, and Anthropic clients and Ollama and vLLM backends, weighted deficit round-robin scheduling, per-user quotas, audit logging, and GPU telemetry to its published gateway description. However, neither https://mindrouter.ai/documentation.html nor https://ai4ra.uidaho.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Sheneman_MindRouter_PEARC26_Short_Paper.pdf was retrieved in the supplied research; October 31, 2026 is a project-reported historical review date, not a newly established access date. These capabilities consequently remain attributed descriptions rather than independently measured facts, and cross-campus federation, frontier routing, and hierarchical financial reservations are hypothetical extensions rather than verified MindRouter features [C2, C5; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:cc14dcfd-f2c0-41f7-92f4-768a1511e7f2].
The comparison distinguishes campus-local inference only, approved frontier API access only, a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and a proposed federation added to that gateway arrangement. The gateway-plus-separate-access baseline matters because it tests whether cooperation contributes something unavailable through a smaller set of institutional relationships. In a meaningful federation comparison, the gateway and federation alternatives retain identical non-federation entitlements, local allocations, and paid-access limits; additional partner capacity and its operating and agreement costs are explicitly attributed to federation. Separately approved external access is not automatically financially mediated and therefore cannot inherit a hard-cap guarantee from approval alone [C2, C5; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:1e89585c-2b17-49bd-bb1e-dc1df98ba8ca; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da].
The proposed workload spans classroom coding, routine analysis, demanding scientific reasoning, multimodal or long-context work, agentic workflows, sensitive analysis, and reproducible batches. Its unit of analysis is a registered parent research task with an input snapshot, requested output, policy classification, acceptance rubric, deadline, provenance requirements, and total resource envelope; calls, tools, retries, and reproducibility repetitions are descendants rather than additional successes. The outcome oracle requires authorized processing and satisfaction of prespecified quality, deadline, and provenance requirements, with correct refusals reported separately from delivered research results. A bounded 90-day pilot is proposed as an execution horizon, not reported as completed work [C4, C5; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:1e89585c-2b17-49bd-bb1e-dc1df98ba8ca; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da].
Evaluation is intended to combine held-out coding tests, known-result and unit checks for analysis, blinded faculty review of scientific reasoning, labeled multimodal fixtures, and agent root-goal and tool-boundary checks. Faculty and students must approve the quality rubrics, latency targets, provenance requirements, and accessibility needs before those criteria can support a service judgment. Overall completion coverage must retain submitted demand in its denominator, while model-quality comparisons use matched policy-eligible strata; otherwise an API-only alternative could appear favorable simply by excluding restricted work. Sensitive-task coverage must also remain separately visible, because the recorded inference from aggregate local usefulness to sensitive-stratum feasibility was withdrawn [C4, C5; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4].
Campus sovereignty enters the proposed design as an eligibility constraint before any destination is ranked by quality, deadline feasibility, or cost. The home campus determines identity, classification, approved processing domains, retention, contracts, audit requirements, and available quota, including restrictions inherited by prompts, outputs, logs, embeddings, and caches. Ambiguous classification supplies no execution destination until resolved, and failure, overload, cancellation, or a better model score supplies no new authority to disclose content. This is an enforcement requirement across tools, network egress, storage, and logging, not a property established by a routing recommendation [C1–C3; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:cc14dcfd-f2c0-41f7-92f4-768a1511e7f2].
The first conditional proposition identifies a limit on disconnected authorization. Suppose a remote admission point has an earlier campus approval, receives no subsequent authoritative information through any channel, and considers a fresh request without a previously committed request-specific grant. Its observations are identical whether the home campus leaves approval unchanged or revokes it during the partition; admitting in the former execution therefore also admits in the latter under the same decision rule. Under this model, availability for fresh disconnected admissions conflicts with immediate revocation enforced at the authorization-decision ordering point, including for randomized rules when safety requires zero unauthorized-admission probability [C1; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:bfcf812f-b3a9-46ff-862c-4d0b4cd76978].
The strongest objection to a broad reading of that proposition is that not every federated request is freshly authorized while disconnected. A reachable home authority removes the missing-information premise, while a campus-approved lease explicitly permits weaker revocation timing; a cache lifetime alone does not establish such permission. An earlier request-specific grant also differs from fresh admission, and authorization before revocation can permit later transmission under decision-time semantics. Thus the recorded limitation neither identifies a MindRouter defect nor establishes a guarantee against post-revocation delivery, execution, or retention [C1, C4; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:bfcf812f-b3a9-46ff-862c-4d0b4cd76978; session:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc].
The second proposition concerns financial exposure rather than data-export authority. For each applicable user, project, department, and campus scope, the proposed ledger maintains the invariant $L_s+H_s\leq B_s$, where $L_s$ denotes settled charges, $H_s$ unresolved reservations, and $B_s$ the cap. Conditional preservation requires complete mediation of billable actions, enforceable finite liability bounds, and durable atomic reservations across all scopes; settlement replaces a hold only with an authoritative final charge no greater than that hold. Timeout or cancellation alone leaves possible liability unresolved, so releasing its reservation can admit additional spending against money already exposed [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:cc14dcfd-f2c0-41f7-92f4-768a1511e7f2].
The related enforcement proposition adds assumptions that cannot be inferred from root-task accounting alone: a linearizable home authority, trusted binding of immutable request content, mediated one-use issuance, and campus acceptance of decision-time authorization. The missing historical originals prevent endorsement of an exact correspondence between the successive specifications, while the supplied excerpts support treating these controls as proposed premises rather than implemented properties. In particular, replay isolation after recovery does not itself preserve financial exposure, and a current zero-charge observation does not establish finality if an earlier sender can still incur liability. These objections narrow the conditional argument without implying that reservations, quotas, or scheduling weights establish availability or starvation freedom [C2, C3; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:296ee708-59eb-4fac-afc4-0d5448ac2d1a].
Service evidence and safety evidence consequently require different evaluators. A paired task comparison can assess useful-completion differences only within its declared population and sampling model; shared queues, outages, caches, budgets, and repeated templates can make nominal repetitions correlated rather than independent observations. Such comparisons do not establish sender safety, which instead requires inspection and fault-injection evidence at authorization, egress, recovery, and billing boundaries. The supplied research contains conditional arguments and hypothetical counterexamples, not observed workload benchmarks or deployment incidents demonstrating federation reliability; even finite tests with no observed violations would provide bounded support rather than universal safety [C3, C4; session:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc].
Finally, the recorded zero-additional-usable-peer-capacity scenario defines a boundary case, not a general finding against federation. Equal useful service opportunities, no offsetting benefit, and strictly positive federation overhead favor the non-federated gateway only under those stipulated conditions. Establishing an improvement requires campus measurements of policy-permitted, model-compatible, reachable, deadline-feasible partner service, together with useful-task outcomes and non-overlapping cost evidence that includes administrative obligations. The evidence therefore changes the central question from whether campuses can pool GPUs to whether cooperation adds useful, authorized service beyond campus provision and separately approved external access without weakening the authority and liability conditions that make that service acceptable [C5; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4].

Recorded findings and observations
The recorded findings narrow the case for sovereign AI access by showing that aggregate completion rates, authorization decisions and budget invariants establish different—and non-interchangeable—properties. The strongest substantive correction concerns sensitive workloads: an acceptable overall local completion fraction does not establish useful service for campus-only tasks. The other findings identify conditional accounting guarantees and counterexamples to stronger authorization, recovery and scheduling claims, rather than demonstrated federation performance. [C6–C10]
The published-capability boundary is consequential to interpreting these findings. The supplied description attributes OpenAI/Ollama/Anthropic client translation, Ollama/vLLM backends, WDRR scheduling, per-user quotas, audit logging and GPU telemetry to MindRouter’s campus gateway; these are descriptions of published claims, not independently measured capabilities in the supplied record. The documentation and PEARC’26 short paper were not retrieved for this investigation, so the reported starting-reference review date of 2026-10-31 is not a newly established access date, and no changed claim has been verified. Cross-campus federation, frontier routing and hierarchical financial reservations therefore remain proposed extensions, not findings about existing MindRouter functionality. [C7, C8]
The numerical findings concern a hypothetical monthly workload of $2{,}000$ root research tasks, including $200$ sensitive campus-only tasks and $1{,}800$ API-eligible tasks. The four alternatives are campus-local inference only, $L$; approved frontier API only, $A$; a campus gateway with separately approved external access, $G$; and a hypothetical federation extension, $F$. The worksheet assumes deadline-feasible local admission of $1{,}000$ roots, allocated to $200$ sensitive and $800$ other tasks under $G$. These quantities specify a scenario, not observed campus demand, measured capacity or institutional policy. [C6, C8]
The unit of useful service is a completed root research objective, not an inference call, retry or agent exchange. In the repaired worksheet, usefulness is the joint event that processing is authorized and the final result meets its quality, deadline and provenance requirements; its probability is not obtained by multiplying independently assumed component probabilities. The proposed evaluation uses task-specific tests, numerical checks or expert assessment, with two blinded assessors and a third adjudicator for disagreements, but these evaluators have not produced recorded outcomes. The separate bounded pilot has a proposed horizon of $90$ days; it must not be confused with an executed validation of the monthly worksheet. [C6, C10]
For a stratum and destination, let $n_{bds}$ denote distinct admissions under baseline $b$, and let $q_{bds}$ denote their average marginal probability of joint usefulness. Expected useful completions are then $\mu_b=\sum_{s,d}n_{bds}q_{bds}$. This expectation identity does not require independent task outcomes, but neither does it establish a probability of meeting a realized service target. Calls and reproducibility repeats remain descendants of their parent task, while correct policy refusal is recorded separately rather than counted as delivered research. [C6, C10]
The sensitive-coverage correction follows directly from disaggregating the local expectation. With the stated admissions, aggregate local usefulness of $0.80$ imposes only $200q_{G,local,S}+800q_{G,local,N}=800$, where $S$ and $N$ distinguish sensitive and other local tasks. It does not identify either stratum’s usefulness: complete failure on sensitive work can coexist with the stipulated aggregate if every other admitted local task succeeds. Sensitive-first admission does not remove this counterexample, because priority determines access to execution rather than the usefulness of the resulting artifacts. The earlier unqualified inference from aggregate local usefulness to sensitive feasibility is therefore withdrawn. [C6]
The repaired sensitive gate requires an additional stratum-specific premise. When $n_{G,local,S}=200$, the expected target $\mu_{G,S}\ge160$ requires $q_{G,local,S}\ge0.80$; with only $a_S\le200$ sensitive admissions, the condition becomes $a_Sq_{G,local,S}\ge160$ and is impossible when $a_S<160$. A simple repair sets both local stratum probabilities to $0.80$, preserving the worksheet’s $800$ expected local completions. More generally, preserving that aggregate while satisfying the sensitive threshold requires $q_{G,local,N}=(800-200q_{G,local,S})/800$. The accompanying table illustrates how the same aggregate can conceal failure or satisfaction of the sensitive gate. [C6]
This repair changes the logical status of feasibility, not the empirical evidence for it. The assumed sensitive probability is additional and hypothetical; it is neither implied by aggregate local performance nor established by allocating all sensitive roots locally. Consequently, an expected-count calculation cannot support a realized coverage guarantee or a campus-specific least-cost-feasible designation. Evidence capable of supporting that designation would need admissions and joint useful completions by classification and task stratum, including failures, deadline misses and unserved demand, rather than only an overall success fraction. [C6]
The accounting finding is similarly conditional but addresses a different outcome. For each applicable budget scope $s$, the abstract ledger maintains settled charges $L_s$ and unresolved reservations $H_s$ under cap $B_s$, with $L_s+H_s\le B_s$. Atomic admission preserves the inequality by reserving a finite enforceable maximum charge $r$ before dispatch; authoritative settlement replaces that reservation with final liability $z$, where $0\le z\le r$, so covered exposure cannot increase. This reasoning requires complete mediation of billable paths, durable concurrent updates and enforceable charge bounds, including tools, retries and separately approved accounts. It is an abstract allocation guarantee, not verification of provider billing or an implemented financial control. [C7, C10]
The timeout counterexample establishes why unresolved liability cannot be released merely because a client stops waiting or requests cancellation. Under a cap of $10$, releasing an ambiguous liability of $6$ and admitting another liability of $6$ permits total exposure of $12$. A separate recovery counterexample shows that fresh identifiers and credentials do not repair a stale backup that has forgotten the first liability: replay isolation and preservation or conservative reconstruction of outstanding exposure are both necessary. These are hand-worked counterexamples to unsafe rules, not saved operational incidents or executed fault-injection results. [C9, C10]
Final reconciliation also has to account for delayed sending, not merely charges observed so far. Under decision-time authorization, a grant committed before revocation or cancellation may still lead to later transmission, receipt or execution; the recorded design does not establish a stronger post-revocation non-delivery guarantee. Thus a current zero-charge report, local silence or an ended invoice period cannot by itself justify releasing exposure while an authorized continuation can still incur liability. The missing operational final-liability criterion and incomplete earlier transition definitions prevent treating the proposed refinement as a verified implementation, even where its local accounting arithmetic is sound. [C9]
The scheduling findings impose another important separation: WDRR weights do not establish unconditional latency bounds or starvation freedom. The recorded counterexamples preserve the need to examine compatible capacity, model and memory fit, blocking, cold starts and actual admitted workload rather than treating scheduling weights as a service guarantee. Likewise, retaining uncertain financial holds can protect caps while reducing availability and fairness. Budget preservation and fair useful access therefore require separate evaluation; neither follows from the other. [C7, C10]
The proposed federation-benefit gate was deliberately strengthened, rather than retrospectively credited with a stronger conclusion. A point estimate of at least $10$ percentage points with an interval excluding zero supports a different proposition from a confidence interval whose lower endpoint exceeds $0.10$; only the latter is the proposed gate for evidence of a benefit exceeding that threshold. The exploratory target of $30$ parent tasks per stratum was not justified as a precision target, and no completed power calculation establishes an affordable enrollment size for the revised gate. Its probability interpretation depends on independent, identically sampled parent-task pairs within the declared population; correlated repetitions do not enlarge that sample, and an independent-block analysis would target a separately defined block-average effect. [C10]
The resulting change to the central argument is substantive: aggregate throughput is no longer sufficient evidence of acceptable sovereign research access. The numerical case now requires explicit sensitive-stratum usefulness, the budget case requires preserved unresolved exposure, and the authorization case stops at an accepted decision-time ordering point. A claimed federation improvement would therefore have to appear as additional policy-permitted, rubric-passing, on-time and provenance-complete root outcomes under matched entitlements, with measured costs and separate safety and service gates—not as nominal GPU capacity, repeated calls or a stronger interpretation of the earlier aggregate worksheet. [C6–C10]

Research methods and proposed approaches
The proposed method separates two requirements that the federation argument must satisfy: useful research completion at the task level and valid authorization at the dispatch level. It compares campus-local inference, approved frontier API access, a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and a hypothetical federation added to that gateway arrangement. The recorded work comprises workload specification, conditional scenario calculations, an abstract transactional design and hand-worked adversarial schedules; it does not comprise an executed comparative benchmark or a deployed federation. Claims C12 and C11 respectively define the evaluation unit and the authorization semantics needed to make that comparison meaningful.
The common workload is a hypothetical monthly cohort of $100$ distinct active researchers submitting $2{,}000$ root tasks. The accompanying table specifies the task allocation, proposed deadlines and acceptance instruments. These are disjoint accounting bins rather than a taxonomy in which sensitivity is a separate technical capability: a coding or analysis task can also carry restrictions, but each root is counted only once in this scenario. The monthly horizon governs workload accounting; annual equivalents assume $12$ identical months and do not constitute additional observations (C12).
A useful completion must jointly satisfy authorization, the end-to-end deadline, the frozen task rubric and the absence of critical factual or security errors. Held-out tests, known-result checks, blinded scientific review and labeled fixtures provide task-specific outcome oracles; agentic work additionally requires root-goal and tool-boundary checks. Batch acceptance requires complete provenance and an explicitly specified numerical tolerance, not an assumption that an API can reproduce an output bit for bit. Correct refusals are recorded separately from delivered research, and the deadlines, rubrics and evaluator arrangements remain subject to campus approval (C12).
The accounting hierarchy distinguishes a root research task, $R$, from a logical inference or tool operation, $O$, and a potentially billable invocation attempt, $A$. Calls, paired Investigator/Skeptic exchanges and retries remain descendants of their original root rather than becoming additional useful completions. This distinction also prevents workflow amplification from disappearing from the comparison: additional calls can increase resource use and financial exposure without increasing the number of completed research goals (C11; C12).
Policy eligibility is assessed separately from technical suitability and deadline-feasible capacity. In the illustrative approval matrix, all $2{,}000$ roots are permitted locally, $1{,}800$ non-sensitive roots are eligible for a named API destination, and only coding, routine analysis and reproducible batches—$1{,}100$ roots—are eligible for a named partner campus. These are scenario assumptions, not established institutional permissions, and federation does not itself enlarge the approved domain. Model, context and GPU-memory fit therefore remain separate admission questions rather than reasons to relax classification or destination restrictions (C12).
The comparative measurement obligation is to evaluate matched roots across eligible alternatives and report admissions and useful completions by stratum. An earlier inference from aggregate local usefulness to sensitive-task coverage cannot be retained: $800$ successes among $1{,}000$ admitted local roots are compatible with all $200$ sensitive roots failing and all $800$ other roots succeeding. Imposed scenario fractions must consequently be distinguished from estimated probabilities and realized outcomes. Paired sample sizes and uncertainty procedures remain unspecified, and descendant calls or repeated evaluations must not be treated as independent root-task observations (C12).
The proposed authorization design binds each operation to an immutable canonical manifest containing the home issuer, principal, project, purpose, payload and attachments, classification restrictions, destination, requested model/version, tool permissions, retention or contract profile, and resource ceilings. Changing the payload or destination requires a new operation linked to the same root; an approved retry receives a new attempt identifier under an unchanged operation. Idempotency keys are scoped to principal and project, with conflicting manifests rejected rather than merged. Identifiers do not confer authority, and sensitive manifests, status information and deduplication records remain within approved access-controlled domains (C11).
Classification validation precedes dispatch and applies to derived material as well as the initial input. Restricted prompts, outputs, embeddings, logs and caches retain applicable restrictions unless a campus-authorized process changes them; ambiguous classification fails closed. Each tool disclosure is a separate operation, so an agent cannot approve its own destination. Protocol normalization must reject unsupported fields, unenumerated attachments or altered content rather than silently removing semantics that were material to authorization (C11).
The central refinement is a durable home-campus transaction authority that orders policy changes, identity and project revocations, cancellation, attempt transitions and relevant ledger updates. Preliminary approval and financial reservation are advisory with respect to transmission: only the final $\operatorname{CommitDispatch}(A)$ transaction creates a dispatch grant. Its commit point, $L(A)$, rechecks current authority, classification, exact manifest binding, destination and retention approval, model and tool permissions, reservation coverage and the designated controlled egress path. The same transaction records the protected audit event and consumes the attempt's one-use issuance right (C11).
Revocation is defined relative to that commit point, not eventual delivery. An applicable revocation $R_v$ ordered before $L(A)$ must prevent the grant; if $L(A)$ precedes $R_v$, the grant authorizes only that immutable attempt, including possible later transmission by its already-designated sender. A revocation-effective acknowledgement is returned only after the revocation commits, so an invocation beginning after that acknowledgement cannot be ordered before it; a partitioned replica cannot create new grants from cached approval. This is explicitly decision-time authorization, and a campus requiring no transmission, execution or retention after revocation must reject these semantics or require additional recipient-side enforcement (C11).
Financial admission is coupled to the same authority without being confused with task success. For each applicable user, project, department, campus or shared-payer scope $k$, the design maintains a cap $B_k$, settled charges $S_k$ and outstanding reservations $H_k$; reserving attempt $A$ atomically requires $S_k+H_k+q_{A,k}\leq B_k$ in every scope, where $q_{A,k}$ is an enforceable maximum liability. A failed check changes none of the scopes. The liability bound must cover all attributable charges, and every spending path—including tools, retries and separately approved API accounts—must be mediated; an unknown bound blocks paid dispatch, while scheduling priority cannot override admission checks (C11).
Uncertainty after commitment preserves the reservation rather than restoring spending headroom. A crash, ambiguous charged timeout, lost response or provider rejection without final billing evidence does not justify retransmitting the same attempt or releasing its unresolved liability. Cancellation closes the operation to future grants and requests best-effort termination, but does not establish remote cancellation or a refund; a paid retry requires explicit approval, fresh checks and a new reservation, and is blocked while its predecessor's liability remains unresolved. Before commitment, release is permitted only through a transition that permanently prevents the attempt from committing; afterward, release depends on authoritative reconciliation or durably fenced proof of non-issuance (C11).
The analytical method is transition induction over this abstract machine. Starting within every cap, atomic reservation preserves the accounting inequality, dispatch commitment leaves reserved liability unchanged, and bounded settlement replaces a reservation with a charge no greater than its ceiling. Authorization preservation likewise depends on final commitment being the sole grant-creation transition and on the controlled adapter enforcing one-use issuance without uncertain retransmission. These are conditional arguments: complete mediation, trustworthy classification bindings, linearizability, crash-safe issuance, sender fencing and reconciliation are implementation premises, not properties established merely by declaring database states (C11).
The proposed fault-injection component uses adversarial event schedules and independent packet observation to test the gap between transactional state and actual egress. Cases include stale credentials, ambiguous classification, malicious-tenant bypass, concurrent revocation or cancellation, WAN partition, replay, simultaneous reservations, crashes, provider rejection, ambiguous timeouts, duplicate settlement and contradictory billing evidence. Discriminating failures are a grant ordered after an applicable revocation, duplicate issuance, unauthorized boundary crossing or liability exceeding a cap. The recorded schedules provide expected outcomes rather than executed results; they also expose the intended availability cost of retaining unresolved reservations, which can persist indefinitely (C11).
The broader empirical proposal is a reviewed $90$-day pilot measuring matched root-level quality, provenance, workflow amplification, memory and context fit, queue and cold-start tails, deadline attainment, unserved demand, availability, fairness and administrative effort. Its sample sizes, uncertainty procedures and stop/go or inconclusive thresholds have not been approved, and no pilot outcomes are recorded. Evidence for an improvement would therefore require both independently inspected enforcement and comparative root-level measurements, rather than a higher assumed usefulness fraction or a larger pool of nominally available GPUs (C11; C12).
Together, these methods change the federation argument from a capacity-sharing proposition into a constrained comparison of completed research goals. Federation must demonstrate additional policy-permitted, rubric-passing, deadline-meeting roots relative to campus service with separately approved external access, while preserving the chosen authorization ordering and all applicable liability caps. More calls, cached approvals or nominal partner eligibility cannot substitute for that evidence (C11; C12).

Evidence limitations and uncertainties
The evidence limits the central argument at the boundary between conditional design claims and demonstrated campus service. The supplied material supports examining when authorization, liability accounting and useful-completion comparisons would be valid; it does not establish that those conditions hold in a deployment. Missing source evidence, unimplemented enforcement and absent workload measurements are therefore distinct limitations, rather than interchangeable reasons for uncertainty. None alone demonstrates that federation is infeasible, but together they preclude a finding that it provides useful, reliable access within campus policy and budget constraints. [C13–C17]
The proposed evaluation defines the workload as registered parent tasks spanning coding, routine analysis, scientific reasoning, multimodal or long-context work, agentic workflows and reproducible batches, with sensitive tasks retaining their additional domain restrictions. A parent task—not an individual model call, retry or reproducibility repeat—is the unit of analysis; paired comparison additionally requires permission to evaluate that task in both the gateway-plus-approved-external-access and federation arms. The proposed outcome oracle requires the final artifact to satisfy a frozen quality rubric, authorized processing, required provenance and an end-to-end deadline. Two blinded assessors would score artifacts independently, with a third adjudicating disagreement, within a proposed $90$-day study horizon whose enrollment must leave time for delivery and grading. These definitions delimit the intended reliability question, but the task-specific pass rules, approved thresholds and executable study protocol remain incomplete. [C16]
Source uncertainty prevents treating the motivating platform description as an independently established capability inventory. The supplied evidence does not contain inspected MindRouter documentation or the PEARC’26 paper, verified access dates and versions, dated API prices, enforceable charging terms, retention commitments or institutional approvals. Consequently, neither the feature descriptions nor any claim that they remain current can be certified from this material. Federation and frontier routing remain hypothetical extensions, not demonstrated MindRouter capabilities; the absence of source verification is a gap in the comparison, not evidence that the published descriptions are false. [C13]
The strongest interpretation of the enforcement argument is conditional: atomic transactions, complete financial mediation, trusted request binding and controlled one-use issuance could support the stated accounting and authorization properties if their premises were implemented. The corresponding objection is operational rather than merely documentary: concurrent authorization, revocation, dispatch, recovery and nested reservations have not been implemented, independently inspected or approved. A database state transition does not itself establish that a sender cannot replay a request, that every spending credential is mediated, or that an external endpoint observes the required retention and charging limits. Moreover, unavailable earlier state and transition definitions prevent checking the claimed exact correspondence between the proposed implementation model and its abstract specification. [C14, C17]
Revocation illustrates why the contractual meaning of a guarantee matters as much as its mechanism. The proposed specification orders revocation against the final authorization decision: revocation ordered first blocks a new grant, whereas a previously committed grant can permit its designated sender to transmit later. That is not a guarantee of no transmission, execution or retention after revocation. Likewise, the partition argument concerns fresh disconnected authorization without authoritative updates or an earlier request-specific grant; it does not establish the same restriction for reachable home-campus decisions or for an explicitly weaker revocation contract. Campus acceptance of these distinctions remains absent. [C14]
The analytical counterexamples below identify failures of particular shortcuts, rather than observed incidents or refutations of every reservation-based design. Their importance is that replay control, financial exposure and authorization ordering cannot substitute for one another. A repaired specification must preserve each relevant property simultaneously. [C14]
Even a corrected reservation argument depends on an operational test for final liability. A current zero-charge report, a quiet local log or the close of an invoice period does not establish that a delayed sender or already-transmitted request can no longer incur charges. Conversely, retaining the full unresolved liability avoids restoring unsupported spending headroom but can impair availability and fairness if uncertainty persists indefinitely. Thus hard-budget preservation cannot be assessed independently of timeout reconciliation, provider contracts and the service consequences of conservative holds. [C14]
The service and economic evidence is weaker than the conditional accounting evidence. Actual costs, model fit, usable capacity, workflow call amplification, latency distributions, sensitive-task usefulness and unserved demand remain unmeasured in the supplied material. The requested four-baseline comparison is therefore incomplete: an illustrative aggregate budget or admission calculation cannot rank campus-local inference, approved API access, a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and federation on quality, coverage and cost. Procurement conclusions also depend on distinguishing sunk, incremental and fully loaded costs, including replacement timing, power and cooling, storage and networking, support, procurement and staff effort. Without that ledger, an apparent saving has no established campus-specific accounting basis. [C15]
The recorded case against federation is correspondingly narrow. If federation adds no usable peer service, preserves identical non-federation entitlements and useful service opportunities, supplies no offsetting benefit and incurs positive overhead, then the gateway alternative is cheaper on the stated cost basis. That conditional comparison is not evidence that campuses typically have no useful capacity to share. Nominal GPU capacity is nevertheless insufficient evidence of benefit: the capacity must also be authorized, reachable, compatible with the requested model and feasible within the task deadline. Both federation superiority and a general claim of its economic disadvantage exceed the available observations. [C15]
Aggregate usefulness also leaves a consequential coverage ambiguity. A total expected-completion target does not establish that sensitive tasks receive useful service, because favorable outcomes in another stratum can conceal failure in the sensitive stratum. The recorded repair supplies explicit stratum-level usefulness assumptions, but those assumptions remain hypothetical rather than measured. Expected counts likewise do not guarantee realized delivery, and constant assumed success fractions cannot establish performance as queues, capacity or paid-access limits become binding. The next decision is whether federation can demonstrate an additional, policy-permitted research capability that justifies extending the trust boundary beyond campus service plus separately approved external access. The unresolved comparison is therefore not simply between more and fewer GPUs: it must establish whether approved remote capacity produces useful completions that the simpler arrangement cannot deliver, after accounting for service losses and administrative overhead. The release of uncertain reservations requires an equally explicit operational rule. A timeout, cancellation acknowledgement or current report of zero charges cannot establish final liability if an already authorized sender could still act later. Before restoring spending headroom, the implementation must demonstrate terminalization of that possibility or obtain an authoritative, enforceable final-liability guarantee covering delayed requests and every applicable financial scope. Procurement review is therefore part of instantiating the hard-budget premise, not merely a later purchasing task; the same requirement applies to separately approved API accounts if their spending is included in the claimed cap. Registered coding tests, numerical checks, expert scientific assessment, task-specific multimodal references, agent objective and tool-boundary checks, and batch rerun tolerances provide distinct evaluators for these requirements. The proposed human evaluation uses two blinded assessors with a third adjudicator for disagreement; latency includes approval handling, queues, cold starts, tools, retries and recovery, rather than ending at the first streamed token. [C18]
The pilot also needs an approved population and uncertainty procedure before it can support a federation-benefit claim. The paired gateway–federation comparison should retain identical non-federation entitlements and a common registered denominator, treating queue rejection, model-fit failure, budget exhaustion and outages as outcomes rather than favorable exclusions. Repeated executions of the same parent task must not be counted as independent observations; independent-pair calculations require the declared sampling assumptions, while an independent-block analysis targets a block-average effect that need not equal the task-weighted effect. The proposed stronger decision rule requires a confidence-interval lower endpoint exceeding $0.10$ for the absolute useful-completion gain, not merely a point estimate of that size with an interval excluding zero. In particular, sensitive-task usefulness requires its own evidence: aggregate local success cannot establish sensitive coverage, and the repaired expected-count scenario is not a realized-service guarantee. Measurements should distinguish policy eligibility, actual admission and useful completion by classification and task requirements, alongside heterogeneous GPU and context fit, compatible capacity, queue and cold-start tails, peak demand, maintenance, and batch versus interactive service. Fairness assessment still requires an approved reference allocation and observation window; scheduling weights alone do not establish starvation freedom. [C18]

[C18, C19, C20]

[C18, C20, C22]

The evidence thus changes the central argument from a presumption that pooled capacity improves access to a requirement that federation earn its additional trust boundary through demonstrated useful service within approved policy and budget constraints.
