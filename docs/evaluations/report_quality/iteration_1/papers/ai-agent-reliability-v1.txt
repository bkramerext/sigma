Problem framing
The investigation asks: Autonomous AI agents are increasingly expected to complete complex tasks involving planning, reasoning, external tools, persistent state, and interaction with changing environments. Demonstrations and benchmark results show that agents can perform many such tasks, but provide limited evidence that they remain dependable across repeated executions, unfamiliar conditions, long-running workflows, and settings where mistakes have significant consequences. This project asks how autonomous and semi-autonomous agents can be evaluated and engineered to deliver reliable, correct, recoverable, and reproducible results across extended, multi-step tasks.

Background and research context
Agent reliability is investigated here as dependable achievement of specified outcomes, rather than as the ability to produce an apparently successful execution. The research question concerns autonomous and semi-autonomous systems that combine planning, reasoning, external tools, persistent state, and interaction with changing environments. Its central distinction is between demonstrating that an agent can perform a task and establishing how dependably it satisfies that task’s requirements under a defined execution protocol. This framing makes the relationship among outcome measurement, failure recovery, and engineering intervention the organizing problem of the investigation. [C1; project:da4580f5-7620-49b0-882e-aee34fcdc05d; strategy:b89a50a8-a730-4ca7-a99f-9eb199aa24d1]
Three complementary lines of inquiry define the scope. Measurement asks how correctness, robustness, and dependable behavior can be distinguished from superficial completion; failure analysis asks how faults propagate through extended execution and which outcomes remain detectable or recoverable. The engineering inquiry considers mechanisms such as constrained tool interfaces, explicit state and provenance, bounded retries, independent verification, escalation, and safe stopping as candidates for comparison, not as preferred solutions. Together, these questions require attention both to what a system accomplishes and to the conditions under which a proposed safeguard could improve its outcomes. [C1; strategy:b89a50a8-a730-4ca7-a99f-9eb199aa24d1; strategy:843ffb99-1d7b-476b-973f-845d79d4b6e3; strategy:260e90ee-69ee-4e5a-b9e8-c0bc84062c81]
A concrete starting point is reliability under a fixed task distribution and execution protocol. The supplied formulation fixes a task distribution $D$, model and prompt versions, tool interfaces, environment-generation policy, and execution budget; a sampled task receives a prespecified batch of $r$ runs, from which an index $J$ is selected uniformly. If $Y_J$ indicates satisfaction of independently specified end-state requirements, safety constraints, and the deadline, the target is $p=\mathbb{E}[Y_J]$. This target concerns success per attempted execution under that batch protocol: timeouts and crashes remain attempts, and the quantity is not success conditional on automated acceptance or success after unlimited retries. [C1; artifact:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f:df5067f1-d298-4203-a108-a2e7056af576]
The distinction between actual rubric satisfaction and an evaluator’s acceptance decision is therefore foundational. The measurement formulations treat actual correctness, automated acceptance, and reference certification as separate variables, rather than defining correctness through agreement between evaluators. Reference validity is itself an evidential requirement: agreement alone cannot establish the proposed allowances for false certification or missed true successes. The background question is consequently not only how to estimate a success rate, but also what outcome evidence makes that rate interpretable as reliability. [C1; artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:0310e2ff-4aa0-4c95-b1a8-795559e484b8; artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:c8070adb-7c30-44b1-b0ad-e838053aaf29]
A deliberately narrow external-action model makes this problem concrete without presuming a general account of agent failure. Consider a task requiring exactly one irreversible external effect, with effect count $N$ and correctness condition $N=1$. The proposed recovery setting distinguishes a request lost before commitment from a committed request whose acknowledgment is lost, while assuming that both leave the agent with identical accessible observations. This example directs attention to the boundary between local execution state and external outcomes: a completion record is an assertion to assess, not a substitute for inspecting the actual effect. [C1; artifact:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f:984e8fd3-f76b-46fb-b868-378c2f103425; artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:4435b6ec-fefc-43a1-8706-a3a6794dbbe5]
Even within this restricted setting, safety, completion, and truthful reporting require different definitions. Preventing multiple effects does not by itself require an effect to occur by a deadline, while verified completion additionally requires the stipulated evidence and reporting behavior. The temporal formulation also separates correctness at an observation horizon from truth when a success report was emitted; a missing or indeterminate report can fail a completion requirement without constituting a false success assertion. These distinctions establish the vocabulary needed to assess recovery without treating harm containment, task achievement, and reporting correctness as interchangeable. [C1; artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:6c3a675d-0b11-40f4-8ca2-548f100367a4; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:36ec8074-02b1-4343-92f5-9ee5dccbf79c]
The engineering question is correspondingly comparative rather than architectural in the abstract. The proposed comparisons fix tasks, model and prompt versions, tool access, environment snapshots, and compute, time, and monetary caps, while separating the systems’ external state and randomizing execution order where necessary. Verification and recovery consume the same constrained resources as ordinary execution, and nonexecution or budget exhaustion remains in the evaluation denominator. These controls define what evidence of an intervention’s benefit would need to distinguish from differences in resources, starting conditions, or selective retention of attempts. [C1; artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:2c4dca26-4c2f-4c55-bab1-b11bb97a5954; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:93f26c0d-5b31-4566-8b43-abc04d7d2cb7]
The analytical scope must nevertheless remain distinct from the broader research ambition. The supplied material provides restricted models, conditional reasoning, and proposed tests, not an executed comparison establishing architectural gains or benchmark-specific reliability. Its external-action boundary concerns actual durable effects rather than dispatch intentions, and does not cover unmodeled downstream consequences; correctness for one such operation is not end-to-end correctness for a dependent workflow. The resulting foundation shifts the central question from whether an agent appears capable to which explicitly scoped outcomes, service conditions, and comparative evidence would justify calling its execution dependable. [C1; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:0040c8af-dd04-481b-8b9a-b08d1be1865e; artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:2a80dd67-72e5-4748-9a2f-3d6ed5bafbf3; artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:fdfb1b57-9f8a-451c-b9b4-2cca9cbf618f]

Recorded findings and observations
The recorded findings contribute a precise distinction between actual task correctness, observable completion signals, and the service conditions that make recovery safe. They are principally analytical results and proposed test specifications: the supplied evidence does not contain an implemented service audit, executed fault-injection study, or controlled agent comparison establishing an engineering benefit. The configuration observation registered as C2 records a previous reasoning-effort setting of “high”; it is not a measurement of correctness or evidence that additional reasoning improved reliability [C2; project:da4580f5-7620-49b0-882e-aee34fcdc05d; session:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f; session:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807].
The measurement argument defines reliability relative to a fixed execution protocol rather than as an unrestricted property of an agent. A task is sampled from a specified distribution $D$, a prespecified batch of $r$ runs is executed, and an index $J$ is selected uniformly from that batch. With $Y_J=1$ denoting satisfaction of the independently specified end-state requirements, safety constraints, and deadline, the target is $p=\mathbb{E}[Y_J]$. This target concerns an attempted execution under the batch protocol, not success conditional on automated acceptance or after unlimited retries; timeouts and crashes remain attempted executions [artifact:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f:df5067f1-d298-4203-a108-a2e7056af576].
The central measurement deduction is that an automated pass rate does not identify this target. If $Z_J$ denotes automated acceptance, $q=\Pr(Z_J=1)$, $f=\Pr(Z_J=1,Y_J=0)$, and $g=\Pr(Z_J=0,Y_J=1)$, partitioning the possible outcomes gives $p=q-f+g$. Consequently, identical pass rates can coexist with entirely different actual correctness: an oracle that always accepts is compatible with executions that always succeed or always fail. A subsequent formulation makes reference error explicit: with automated acceptance $O$, reference certification $R$, $a=\Pr(O=1,R=0)$, and an assumed allowance $\Pr(O=1,R=1,Y=0)\leq\eta$, the recorded derivation gives $p\geq\max(0,q-a-\eta)$ without assuming independence among the three labels. The allowance is an outcome-validity premise, not something established by agreement between judges [artifact:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f:df5067f1-d298-4203-a108-a2e7056af576; artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:0310e2ff-4aa0-4c95-b1a8-795559e484b8].
For system comparisons, the recorded design treats the sampled task, rather than each repeated run, as the independent sampling unit. Both systems execute their scheduled batches on the same task in isolated copies of the initial environment; dependence among runs, systems, and judges within a task cluster is unrestricted. With prespecified stratum weights $w_h$ and system-specific correctness probabilities $p_{Ah}$ and $p_{Bh}$, the target contrast is $\Delta=\sum_h w_h(p_{Ah}-p_{Bh})$, under independent, representative task clusters. This design preserves the distinction between repeated executions of one task and additional independently sampled tasks, while requiring versions, prompts, tools, horizons, and resource caps to remain fixed for the comparison [artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:2c4dca26-4c2f-4c55-bab1-b11bb97a5954; artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:c8070adb-7c30-44b1-b0ad-e838053aaf29].
A separate finite-roster argument changes the source of statistical uncertainty. It fixes the scheduled execution slots, their actual outcomes, and the observation horizon before random audit selection, retaining every scheduled slot under a prespecified nonexecution rule. The recorded argument uses hypergeometric inversion for marginal count bounds and a union bound for simultaneous coverage; its probability statement concerns audit selection conditional on the frozen roster, so it does not require independent execution outcomes. This is a different inferential target from population reliability: its conservative use of possible-outcome sets depends on those sets containing the actual outcomes, and it supplies no automatic transport from the audited roster to deployment [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:f59b1252-c29e-4f4a-a888-f436ab9a93aa; session:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523].
The recovery analysis identifies an information boundary that local reasoning cannot cross under the stipulated observations. One history loses a request before commitment, leaving no external effect; another commits the effect but loses its acknowledgment. If both expose identical information at recovery, and the only terminal actions are to skip or issue one retry that commits an additional effect, a local policy must use the same retry probability $q$ in both histories. Its respective error probabilities are $1-q$ and $q$, giving a sharp worst-case error bound of at least $1/2$, attained by $q=1/2$. This is a bound for a restricted decision model, not a deployment failure estimate; in particular, the recorded correction preserves the retry-commitment premise because issuing a retry does not itself establish that another effect commits [artifact:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f:984e8fd3-f76b-46fb-b868-378c2f103425; session:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f; artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:7d39bad1-603b-43cf-ad04-0d256b837472].
The sufficient recovery mechanism changes that service boundary rather than extracting additional information from the same ambiguous local state. It preserves a stable operation key and immutable payload, and atomically couples creation of the actual durable effect with creation of its completion record. Starting from empty effect and completion state, the first absent-key transition creates both, whereas a repeated matching request returns the retained result without another effect; payload mismatches are rejected. Under serialization, durability, noncolliding identities, and retention throughout every possible retry or delayed delivery, the recorded invariant establishes at-most-once effects. At least one successful processing transition is additionally necessary for an effect to occur, and delivery of appropriate confirmation is separately necessary for verified completion [artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:4435b6ec-fefc-43a1-8706-a3a6794dbbe5; session:6298e28d-b9e9-4b8a-99cb-a4872c5af875].
The transition specifications make this distinction auditable by separating actual effects, completion records, pending messages, and volatile processing state. Sending changes the pending-message pool without creating an effect; delaying a message leaves it eligible for later processing, whereas dropping it removes it. In the refined model, receiving destructively removes a request into volatile execution state, so a pre-commit crash loses that consumed delivery rather than implicitly arranging redelivery. The displayed manual schedules preserve correspondence between effects and completion records under the atomic contract, but establish neither fairness nor eventual processing for arbitrary executions. Their substantive contribution is therefore a determinate request-lifecycle specification, including outstanding work that can survive a client timeout [artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:4435b6ec-fefc-43a1-8706-a3a6794dbbe5; artifact:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10:9df3cd5f-0a68-4356-81e9-189708026006; session:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10].
Later specifications distinguish safety throughout execution from completion at a deadline. For one stable operation identity, the recorded finite-retention formulation defines safety as $C\leq1$ at every execution prefix, including after the evaluation deadline $H$, while effect completion requires $C(H)=1$. Verified completion further requires authoritative evidence and a truthful report by $H$; the safety condition alone permits permanent omission. The modeled boundary is the actual durable outcome, not an outbox entry or dispatch intention, and does not cover effects outside that boundary or downstream delayed harms [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:6c3a675d-0b11-40f4-8ca2-548f100367a4; artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:2a80dd67-72e5-4748-9a2f-3d6ed5bafbf3].
The reporting arguments add a corresponding evidential distinction: a correct state at the evaluation horizon does not by itself establish that a completion report was truthful when emitted. Report-time truth depends on commits preceding emission, and equal timestamp labels do not establish that ordering without suitable ordering evidence. Likewise, conformance to a conservative evidence-mapping rule implies actual-outcome containment only when the evidence used is truthful, complete where claimed, and correctly scoped and ordered. Taken together, the recorded findings shift the reliability argument from obtaining plausible completion signals to specifying three separately accountable objects: the external outcome, the service contract governing its creation, and the evidence supporting the report [artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:36ec8074-02b1-4343-92f5-9ee5dccbf79c; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:8a554d50-7080-431c-aacf-1fa03496133c; session:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807].

Unsuccessful approaches and competing evidence
The unsuccessful approaches narrow the reliability argument by showing where retry reasoning, duplicate suppression, and outcome labeling fail to establish dependable completion. The recorded preflight error documents an unsuccessful attempt, not evidence that a particular reliability mechanism is ineffective; no complete research artifact accompanies that attempt [C3; session:6298e28d-b9e9-4b8a-99cb-a4872c5af875]. The substantive negative evidence instead consists of explicit counterexamples and corrections to conditional arguments, rather than measured architectural regressions.
The retry argument concerns two histories that leave the agent with the same pending local state: in $W_0$, the original request produces no effect, whereas in $W_1$, it commits once but its acknowledgment is lost. For a task requiring exactly one irreversible effect by deadline $H$, let $q$ denote the probability of issuing a retry under this indistinguishable information. In the restricted construction where an issued retry commits by $H$, external-state correctness is bounded by $q$ in $W_0$ and by $1-q$ in $W_1$, yielding worst-case failure of at least $1/2$. This is a conditional bound for the specified ambiguous-commitment decision, not a deployment failure-rate estimate or a bound covering authoritative lookup, compensation, or other distinguishing observations [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:5e47ab12-4c7d-45ae-9b1f-af87a75768d6; session:6298e28d-b9e9-4b8a-99cb-a4872c5af875].
The attempted extension of that bound to arbitrary retry loss fails. An always-retry policy has $q=1$, but if every retry is dropped, the already-committed history $W_1$ retains exactly one effect; its external-state correctness probability is therefore $1$, contradicting the proposed upper bound $1-q=0$. The correction retracts the relaxed-delivery inequality while retaining the original assured-commitment construction. It supplies no replacement bound for arbitrary delivery and adaptive observations, so retry issuance must not be treated as equivalent to another committed effect [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:7d39bad1-603b-43cf-ad04-0d256b837472; artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:5e47ab12-4c7d-45ae-9b1f-af87a75768d6].
A separate overstatement concerns the claim that splitting effect creation from completion-record creation necessarily defeats at-most-once safety. Under the specified serialized, durable, no-repair contracts, effect-first execution can commit an effect, crash before recording completion, and then create a duplicate on a same-key retry. Marker-first execution has a different failure: a crash after the marker but before the effect causes subsequent same-key requests to return cached success while the effect count remains $C=0$. Restricted marker-first suppression can thus preserve at-most-once safety while losing delivery and invalidating the meaning of its success record; the two write orders do not refute the same property [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:5e47ab12-4c7d-45ae-9b1f-af87a75768d6; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:c1fd1f73-e977-4767-af23-6d44dd28c105; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:3c991003-9fc5-4d55-848a-000e98154d8c].
Regenerating the operation key after ambiguous recovery does not provide a general repair. Although a fresh key can bypass the marker-first omission in the particular intermediate-crash trace, it also bypasses a valid completion record after a fully committed operation whose acknowledgment was lost. Even an implementation that atomically couples each effect with its receipt can then produce one effect under each of two keys, giving $C=2$ for the same logical operation. The counterexample distinguishes per-key duplicate suppression from per-operation correctness and does not refute stronger split-write designs with durable repair mechanisms excluded by the model [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:5e47ab12-4c7d-45ae-9b1f-af87a75768d6].
The atomic, stable-key alternative also supports a narrower conclusion than successful completion. Its conditional invariant prevents duplication without requiring delivery, but eventual effect creation additionally requires continued attempts and a service schedule in which an attempt completes; truthful terminal reporting further requires receipt delivery and client progress. Even those eventual-progress premises impose no finite completion deadline: useful deliveries can occur after any chosen $H$. Accordingly, neither deadline success nor its probability follows from the safety invariant, and the manually specified traces establish no general fairness or eventual-processing property [artifact:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523:5e47ab12-4c7d-45ae-9b1f-af87a75768d6; session:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10].
The outcome rubric contains a further failure that is independent of duplicate suppression: correct external state at the evaluation horizon does not establish truthful completion when the agent reports success. The challenged rubric permits a SUCCESS report by tick $15$ and evaluates effect state at $H=20$, allowing commitment after the report but before the horizon. That history can satisfy horizon-based correctness despite a premature completion claim. The correction therefore separates report-time truth from horizon-time state; it does not, by itself, change the predictions for the five specified schedules [artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:3c991003-9fc5-4d55-848a-000e98154d8c].
The subsequent temporal specification still leaves recoverable labeling gaps. Complete report capture together with exactly one matching effect row is insufficient to certify success if the captured report is INDETERMINATE, late, or accompanied by multiple final reports. Conversely, evidence of one timely matching SUCCESS does not establish uniqueness when capture is incomplete or report closure is absent. Definite success labels therefore require the full reporting contract—content, well-formedness, uniqueness, timeliness, and capture or closure—not merely the presence of a correct effect and a favorable report [artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:36ec8074-02b1-4343-92f5-9ee5dccbf79c].
The proposed ordering ablation also fails to isolate uncertainty unless the retained evidence actually permits both event orders. If trusted timestamps on a common clock still place report emission at tick $15$ and commitment at tick $18$, removing a separate ordering certificate does not make their order unknown. A valid analytical contrast must specify which timestamp, clock-comparability, or ordering evidence is removed and show that both witness histories satisfy what remains. Otherwise, the ablation cannot support its claimed change in the outcome label [artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:36ec8074-02b1-4343-92f5-9ee5dccbf79c].
These counterexamples reject particular inferences, not entire architectural families: issuing a retry need not create an effect, avoiding duplicates need not complete an operation, and obtaining a correct state by a deadline need not vindicate an earlier success report. The supplied record contains no inspected service or observer implementation, executed fault-injection comparison, or measured architectural benefit, so these corrections cannot establish net engineering improvement [artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:36ec8074-02b1-4343-92f5-9ee5dccbf79c]. What they change is the burden of the central reliability argument: any claimed guarantee must identify the property preserved, the delivery and storage assumptions that sustain it, and the reporting evidence needed to distinguish genuine completion from a merely favorable final state.

Evidence limitations and uncertainties
The evidence supports a distinction between conditional analytical results and demonstrated agent reliability, rather than treating the former as evidence of the latter. The central limitation is evidential: reported agreement does not independently verify an argument, its premises, or its applicability to an implemented system. The recorded analyses identify consequences of specified information, service, and evaluation contracts, but they do not establish that real agents and tools satisfy those contracts. This distinction governs how both positive arguments and negative findings can contribute to the paper’s reliability claim [C4; session:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f].
For recovery arguments, the consequential gap lies between the modeled information boundary and the actual external effect. The indistinguishability construction assumes that an uncommitted request and a committed request with a lost acknowledgment leave identical information available to the recovering policy. Timing, tool metadata, restored memory, or outstanding requests could defeat that equivalence in an implementation. Conversely, an apparently successful repair would not establish external correctness unless the effect oracle were independent and complete; no supplied implementation evidence establishes either the observation equivalence or these oracle properties [C4; session:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f].
The sufficient service contracts have a similarly important boundary: the irreversible business effect must share the stipulated atomic, durable transition with its deduplication or authorization state. A key table, retained receipt, or atomic local intent is not by itself evidence that this boundary includes the actual downstream effect. Service atomicity, durability, retention, concurrency behavior, and preservation of operation identity by real agents remain unaudited. The analytical conclusions therefore identify properties that would support restricted recovery guarantees, not measured compliance by existing services or a demonstrated causal improvement in end-to-end reliability [C4; session:a1a9ab51-f8d1-4b60-ba10-baad7585a34f; session:d2f726f2-e5d5-4a00-ac51-a07b089bcf10; session:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523].
Evaluation uncertainty also concerns whether certification tracks truth, not merely whether a statistical calculation is correct. The population-comparison analysis assumes directional allowances for falsely accepted intervention outcomes and falsely rejected baseline successes, but those allowances lack independent outcome calibration. Likewise, conservative outcome sets contain the actual result only if the underlying snapshot, cutoff ordering, report capture, and nonexecution evidence are sound. Separate retention paths and archive digests do not establish those premises, and a silently false certificate can defeat containment despite correct application of the mapping rule. Statistical error control cannot substitute for these missing connections between recorded evidence and actual outcomes [C4; session:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3; session:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807].
The statistical arguments nevertheless have different dependence requirements and should not be dismissed under a single independence objection. The population analysis assumes independent, representative task clusters while allowing dependence within a cluster; the finite-panel audit instead conditions on a frozen roster and locates randomness in uniform audit selection. Its coverage argument therefore does not require independent execution outcomes. This distinction preserves the narrower finite-panel interpretation without establishing transport to a deployment population, and the recorded hand calculations and proposed coverage checks are not executed empirical audits or implementation tests [C4; session:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3; session:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523; session:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807].
Negative findings must likewise be attached to the proposition they challenge. Although one recorded outcome is labeled as a reported counterexample, its detailed assessment treats the finite-panel lemma as convincing under its assumptions and locates the delivery objection in a different recovery claim. That objection restricts removal of the retry-commitment premise; it does not refute the audit lemma. Historical references without substantive source content, incomplete artifacts, and unresolved prior obligations limit stronger compatibility claims, while an unavailable result supplies neither a successful demonstration nor a substantive refutation [C4; session:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523; session:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3; session:6ca303b6-130f-4f30-ad6e-9dea7e2031ff; session:6298e28d-b9e9-4b8a-99cb-a4872c5af875].
Finally, the outcome being guaranteed is narrower than dependable task completion. At-most-once effect safety permits permanent omission, eventual successful delivery does not imply completion by a fixed deadline, and per-operation deduplication does not establish payload correctness, multi-service atomicity, or absence of delayed harm. No controlled evidence measures the associated recovery, completion, latency, storage, or monetary-cost tradeoffs. The evidence thus changes the central argument from an assertion that a reliability mechanism works in practice to a more precise account of which contracts make a restricted guarantee possible and which outcome judgments remain unsupported [C4; session:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3; session:bea430f9-56e7-4ced-aa69-376cee40c523].

Open questions and next steps
The immediate research priority is to establish whether reference adjudication tracks actual task outcomes, rather than merely providing a second completion label. This is the unresolved obligation in C5: a reliability guarantee must either validate reference soundness or explicitly accommodate reference errors. The available work supplies conditional measurement arguments and proposed validation tests, but not an independently audited implementation demonstrating that the reference evidence is sound. The next steps should therefore connect success certification to durable external outcomes before using certification rates to recommend an agent architecture. The reference-error allowance provides a precise target for that validation. Let $Y$ denote actual rubric satisfaction, $O$ automated acceptance, and $R$ reference certification, with $p=\Pr(Y=1)$, $q=\Pr(O=1)$, and $a=\Pr(O=1,R=0)$. The recorded conditional construction assumes $c=\Pr(O=1,R=1,Y=0)\leq\eta$ and obtains $p\geq\max\{0,q-a-\eta\}$. The substantive next step is to justify an upper bound on this target-population joint miscertification probability; it is not interchangeable with a conditional error rate among certified executions. Until independent outcome evidence supports such an allowance, results indexed by $\eta$ should be presented as sensitivity analysis rather than as an unconditional reliability certificate. Comparative evaluations require attention to both directions of adjudication error. Although false reference rejection makes the preceding one-system lower bound conservative, the proposed system comparisons separately require justified directional allowances, particularly for missed true baseline successes. Agreement among judges does not establish either allowance. A calibration study should therefore compare judgments with independently obtained outcome evidence in the intended task population, rather than treating agreement between automated and reference judgments as validation of truth. For the ledger-backed tasks considered here, a useful pilot would first validate the outcome observer. It should capture actual durable effects by logical operation identity, including effects created under regenerated retry keys, rather than relying on completion records or dispatch intentions. It should also validate report capture and the ordering of commits, report events, and evaluation cutoffs; correctness at the horizon and truth at report emission should remain distinct outcomes. Missing or contradictory observations must not silently become either success or evidence that no effect occurred. The table distinguishes the resulting validation questions so that success on one does not substitute for evidence on another. Fault injection should then test whether the observer remains informative at the boundaries where recovery is ambiguous. The proposed harness must demonstrate that pre-commit request loss and post-commit acknowledgment loss expose identical recovery information to the agent while remaining distinguishable through independently audited service state. Tests should distinguish dropped requests from delayed requests, account for outstanding work, and include crashes between acknowledgment receipt and durable client completion. Delivery schedules must remain explicit: issuing a retry does not itself establish that another effect commits, as the dropped-retry counterexample shows. These requirements make the experiment a test of external outcomes rather than a comparison of plausible transcripts. The statistical implementation needs a separate validation programme. Proposed checks include exhaustive enumeration of small audit rosters, census and all-unknown cases, confidence-level sensitivity, correlated outcomes, and outcome-dependent evidence loss. Repeated-panel tests should also exercise task heterogeneity, within-task dependence, and asymmetric judge errors, while retaining every scheduled execution under a prespecified nonexecution rule. Agreement in these finite tests would support correctness for the checked implementation cases; it would not prove general coverage or establish that the outcome sets supplied to the calculation contain the truth. Once the reference evidence and calculation are validated, controlled agent comparisons can address engineering benefit. The proposed design holds tasks, model and prompt versions, tool access, environment snapshots, and execution budgets fixed; isolates system state; and randomizes condition order where appropriate. Comparisons among fencing, read-back-only retry, restart, and escalation should retain nonexecution and budget exhaustion, and report omissions, duplicates, false completion reports, unresolved outcomes, latency, and inclusive cost separately. Deployment fault frequencies and asymmetric harm costs require separate estimation: neither conditional safety traces nor an experimentally selected fault mixture supplies those quantities. [C5; artifact:ebc7ac05-823c-4a02-9590-1e9f9726e807:93f26c0d-5b31-4566-8b43-abc04d7d2cb7; artifact:60b63ed0-9a6f-486c-9d4f-232b1fe082c3:fdfb1b57-9f8a-451c-b9b4-2cca9cbf618f]
Reference validation also needs an explicit response to failures of its own trust assumptions. Cross-execution and cross-build incidents should test the scope of certificate withdrawal and independently justified conditions for restoring certification; locally marking one observation unknown does not establish that other certificates remain sound. Broader applications additionally require outcome evidence for dependent multi-service workflows and delayed harms, rather than extending a single-operation ledger rubric by assertion. These obligations change the central reliability argument from accepting independently named judges to requiring independently justified outcome evidence, with any remaining adjudication uncertainty carried into the reported guarantee.
