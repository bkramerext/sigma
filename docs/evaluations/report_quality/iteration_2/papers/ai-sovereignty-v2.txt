Sovereign AI Federation v2

Abstract
The investigation asks: Can universities with uneven GPU capacity provide useful, reliable AI research access while retaining control of sensitive data and staying within hard budgets? The saved research reports a provisional finding, not independently verified here: Structured argument steps are recorded; review their evidence and objections. The analysis distinguishes recorded arguments from demonstrated results, identifies remaining gaps, and does not claim experiments or proof steps absent from the frozen record.

Problem framing
The investigation asks: Can universities with uneven GPU capacity provide useful, reliable AI research access while retaining control of sensitive data and staying within hard budgets? Compare four baselines: (1) campus-local inference only, (2) approved frontier API only, (3) a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and (4) a proposed cooperative federation among campuses. Background and research context
The research question is whether shared AI infrastructure can expand useful university research access without transferring control over sensitive data or weakening hard budget constraints. The comparison concerns four alternatives: campus-local inference, approved frontier APIs, a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and a proposed cooperative federation. Federation is therefore an option to evaluate against a controlled campus service, not an assumed solution to uneven GPU capacity. Its relevant contribution would be additional policy-permitted research capability, rather than resource sharing alone. [C1]
MindRouter provides the starting campus-gateway context. The supplied project description attributes protocol translation across OpenAI, Ollama and Anthropic clients and Ollama/vLLM backends, WDRR fair-share scheduling, per-user quotas, audit logging and GPU telemetry to its published on-campus service. The starting references are the documentation at https://mindrouter.ai/documentation.html and the PEARC’26 short paper at https://ai4ra.uidaho.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Sheneman_MindRouter_PEARC26_Short_Paper.pdf. However, neither reference was retrieved in the supplied research record: 2026-10-31 is a project-reported historical review date, not an independently established access date, and the feature descriptions remain attributed claims rather than inspected or measured capabilities. Cross-campus federation, frontier routing and the proposed financial enforcement controls are consequently treated as hypothetical extensions, not existing MindRouter features. [C1]
The alternatives differ in execution domains and institutional dependencies, as specified in the comparison table. In particular, separately approved external access need not imply automatic gateway routing: an explicitly authorized portal or account workflow is a distinct baseline. Adding federation to that baseline introduces partner-campus execution without, by itself, enlarging the set of destinations authorized for any particular task. This distinction makes it possible to ask whether cooperation adds a capability unavailable through local service and separately approved external access, rather than attributing every external-model benefit to federation. [C1]
The study defines sovereignty through retained campus authority over identity, classification, approved processing domains, retention, audit requirements, contracts and quotas. Its proposed policy boundary covers derived prompts, outputs, logs, embeddings and caches as well as original inputs; restricted, student, patient, controlled, sponsor-restricted and unpublished content remains within approved domains by default. Ambiguous classification supplies no execution destination until resolved. Quality estimates, available capacity and incremental cost can rank eligible destinations, but overload, outages, retries, timeouts or exhausted budgets cannot create permission to use an otherwise prohibited destination. [C1]
Useful access is defined at the level of an authorized root research task, not an individual model call. Classroom coding, routine analysis, demanding scientific reasoning, multimodal or long-context work, agentic workflows and reproducible batches provide the proposed workload scope, while sensitivity is a classification that must also be tracked within task categories. A useful completion must satisfy prespecified quality, deadline and provenance requirements; tool calls and retries remain descendants of the same task, and a correct policy refusal is recorded separately from delivered research. This accounting basis prevents multi-call workflows from inflating completion counts and keeps restricted-demand coverage visible rather than allowing aggregate performance to stand in for every classification stratum. [C1]
The proposed comparison separates permission to execute from the technical opportunity to complete a task. Relevant technical conditions include GPU memory and model/context fit, compatible capacity, queueing, cold starts, maintenance, peak demand and the allocation of service between interactive and batch work. Latency, throughput, availability, fairness and starvation therefore belong alongside task quality and provenance/version reproducibility in the evaluation scope; scheduling weights alone are not treated as service guarantees. Cost comparisons likewise require a common workload and horizon, with sunk, incremental and fully loaded accounts kept distinct: local ownership and operations include capital, replacement, idle capacity, energy, cooling, storage, networking, support, procurement and staff, whereas paid workflows include their constituent calls, retries, tools, embeddings, egress and contractual commitments. [C1]
The conditional design also makes its authority and accounting assumptions explicit. It assumes a durable, linearizable home-campus authority, trusted binding of classifications to immutable requests, enforceable nested liabilities and complete mediation of invocation and spending paths, including separately approved accounts. Its decision-time authorization semantics permit an earlier committed grant to transmit after a later revocation; they do not establish a stronger prohibition on subsequent delivery or execution, and uncertain transmission is not repaired by reissuing a consumed grant. These assumptions define the proposed extension’s scope rather than demonstrated deployment properties. The evidence thus frames the central question more precisely: federation must add usable, authorized research capability over the gateway-plus-approved-access baseline while preserving the same campus authority and accounting obligations; nominal pooled capacity is not sufficient to establish that advantage. [C1]

Recorded findings and observations
The recorded analysis makes authorized, useful root-task completion—not inference calls, tokens, or nominal GPU capacity—the unit for comparing research access. Its substantive contribution to claim C2 is to separate policy eligibility, actual admission, and successful delivery, and to show that aggregate success can conceal failure for campus-only sensitive work. The supporting results are conditional deductions and hypothetical accounting examples: the supplied evidence contains no measured four-baseline comparison, and neither the starting MindRouter sources nor current provider prices and institutional permissions were independently inspected. Federation and frontier routing therefore remain proposed extensions rather than established MindRouter capabilities. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
A root task is one research objective with a fixed input snapshot, requested artifact, quality rubric, deadline, classification, provenance requirements, and resource envelope. Agent calls, tools, retries, critique exchanges, and reproducibility repeats are descendants of that objective, not additional completed tasks. In the recorded acceptance rule, a task succeeds only when its final artifact satisfies the quality rubric, required provenance, authorized processing, and end-to-end deadline jointly. A correct policy refusal is recorded as safe behavior but not as delivered research; refusals and policy-ineligible demand consequently need separate reporting. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db]
This definition requires task-specific acceptance rather than a common model score. The recorded proposals use held-out tests for coding, known-result and numerical checks for analysis, expert assessment for scientific reasoning, labeled references for multimodal tasks, and root-goal plus tool-boundary checks for agentic workflows. Reproducible batches additionally require a registered rerun or numerical-tolerance criterion; a model-version label alone does not establish reproducibility. Delivery latency begins at submission and includes approval handling, queues, cold starts, tools, retries, and recovery, rather than ending at the first streamed token. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da]
The numerical deadlines are not consistent across the recorded proposals and cannot be treated as a settled service specification. One proposal assigns coding a deadline of $30$ seconds and agentic workflows $900$ seconds; another assigns coding $120$ seconds and agentic workflows $60$ minutes. Both describe illustrative targets awaiting approval, not observations. Because deadline compliance is part of usefulness, changing these values changes the outcome being compared even when the generated artifact is unchanged. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da]
The stratum-specific formulation makes the comparison precise. Let $N_s$ denote offered root tasks in classification-and-task stratum $s$, and let $n_{bds}$ denote distinct roots assigned to destination $d$ under baseline $b$, with $\sum_d n_{bds}\leq N_s$ and unauthorized assignments constrained to $n_{bds}=0$. If $q_{bds}$ is the average marginal probability that those admissions jointly satisfy authorization, quality, deadline, and provenance requirements, expected useful completions are $\mu_b=\sum_{s,d}n_{bds}q_{bds}$. This expectation identity requires no independence assumption, but its probabilities concern the selected workload, load, and configuration rather than universal model success rates. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
The most consequential counterexample concerns sensitive-stratum coverage. For $200$ sensitive and $800$ other local admissions, aggregate expected usefulness of $0.80$ establishes only $200q_S+800q_N=800$. The assignment $q_S=0$ and $q_N=1$ satisfies that equation while delivering no expected sensitive completions; the same counterexample can be expressed as exact scenario counts. Thus neither aggregate usefulness nor sensitive-first admission establishes useful sensitive service, and the recorded analysis explicitly withdraws the earlier unqualified sensitive-feasibility inference. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
The repaired example adds the missing premise rather than inferring it from the aggregate: both local strata have hypothetical joint usefulness of $0.80$, while selected API admissions have hypothetical usefulness of $0.90$. Its common monthly cohort contains $2{,}000$ roots, including $200$ sensitive campus-only roots, and assumes deadline-feasible local admission of $1{,}000$ roots. The illustrative acceptance gates require $160$ expected useful sensitive roots and $1{,}400$ expected useful roots overall. These assumptions produce the comparison in the table, whose federation row is deliberately restricted to the recorded case of no additional usable peer service and no offsetting benefit. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
The repaired scenario exposes different failure modes among the alternatives. Campus-local service misses the overall expected-count gate because admission capacity is limited, whereas API-only service cannot satisfy the campus-only sensitive gate regardless of its aggregate completion count. For the gateway with separately approved external access, let $B$ be the API allocation and $r>0$ an enforceable maximum liability per admitted API root; then $n_G=\min(1000,\lfloor B/r\rfloor)$ and $\mu_G=800+0.90n_G$. The two expected-count gates hold precisely when $\lfloor B/r\rfloor\geq667$, given the stated local-stratum assumptions. This threshold is an expectation calculation, not a guarantee that the realized workload meets either gate. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
The reservation-based admission count also has a narrower interpretation than an API price calculation. It assumes complete financial mediation, durable atomic reservations across applicable quotas, nonbinding subsidiary quotas, no opening liabilities or commitments, adequate API capacity, and no recycling of reservation savings within the fixed plan. The bound $r$ must cover workflow descendants and ancillary liabilities, including retries, tools, embeddings, and cache misses charged at uncached rates. An average observed charge or an optimistic cached-token estimate would not supply the enforceable maximum required by this argument. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]
Comparable denominators are equally important when moving from the scenario to a paired federation comparison. The recorded design distinguishes all requested tasks, authorized tasks, tasks permitted to undergo both workflows, and actual admissions in each workflow. Its primary comparison uses the same fixed paired population for the gateway and federation arms: model-fit failures, queue rejection, exhausted paid allocations, outages, and non-delivery remain outcomes rather than post hoc exclusions. Tasks prohibited from paired evaluation are reported separately with reasons and arm-specific coverage, not exported for comparability; the paired population's share of all requested work exposes the resulting selection. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da]
Within that fixed population, the paired effect is $\Delta=P(Y_F=1,Y_G=0)-P(Y_F=0,Y_G=1)$, where each outcome indicator applies the joint useful-completion rule. This measures federation gains net of tasks it harms, rather than counting only newly successful remote executions. The recorded proposal strengthens the benefit gate from a point estimate of at least $0.10$ with an interval excluding zero to a lower confidence bound exceeding $0.10$; evidence of any positive effect is not evidence that the effect exceeds that threshold. The associated independent-pair calculations are analytical study-design results, with exact power and affordable enrollment unresolved, rather than observed federation benefits. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:2fef1213-38ff-4acf-9af2-a0c28b89b0da]
Finally, useful completions supply the denominator for the recorded economic result. For the same workload, horizon, acceptance rules, and cost basis, let the gateway cost and useful completions be $C_G>0$ and $S_G>0$, and let federation add net cost $\Delta C>0$ and net useful completions $\Delta S$, with $S_G+\Delta S>0$. Federation lowers cost per useful completion exactly when $\Delta S/S_G>\Delta C/C_G$; additional capacity or throughput alone is insufficient. In the restricted case of identical usable service opportunities, no offsetting federation benefit, and positive overhead, the gateway is cheaper. The recorded findings therefore change the central comparison from whether federation pools more resources to whether it delivers enough additional authorized, timely, provenance-complete research outcomes in the required strata to justify its added cost. [C2; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:5c8fdc17-0ce8-4ea3-b8dc-79e05f97d0db; artifact:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc:d802474b-5a2b-4061-867d-be22686d9ce4]

Evidence limitations and uncertainties
The evidence limits the central argument to conditional feasibility: it does not establish that federation delivers useful, reliable research access under actual campus policies and budgets. The principal source-grounding gap is consequential rather than merely bibliographic: published capabilities, current API rates, contractual terms and institutional approvals lack inspected supporting evidence. Conditional accounting and authorization arguments therefore cannot establish that their premises hold for a deployable service. [C3; session:36e06a52-e138-48ee-9ccf-1519bb6faedc]
The supplied characterization of MindRouter includes protocol translation, WDRR scheduling, quotas, audit logging and GPU telemetry, but these descriptions are not independently substantiated by the supplied excerpts. The reported review date of 2026-10-31 is not an independently established access date for either the documentation or the PEARC’26 paper. Federation and frontier routing must consequently remain hypothetical extensions, not attributed product capabilities. The missing source inspection is an evidence limitation, not evidence that the described campus gateway capabilities are absent. [C3]
Missing commercial and institutional evidence also prevents a hard-budget or sensitive-data deployment conclusion. Dated token prices alone would not establish enforceable maximum liability for complete workflows, including retries, tools, embeddings, cache misses and ambiguous charged timeouts; the relevant charging terms remain unestablished. Likewise, destination approval would not by itself establish retention obligations, permission to process derived artifacts, or complete financial mediation. These distinctions separate a proposed admissibility rule from evidence that actual providers and campus agreements satisfy it. [C3]
The financial argument has a specific unresolved recovery weakness, not merely a general need for implementation testing. In the hypothetical stale-backup counterexample, an outstanding liability of $6$ is absent from a restored ledger with a cap of $10$; admitting another liability of $6$ produces total exposure of $12$, even if fresh identifiers prevent replay. Namespace fencing therefore does not alone preserve the budget bound: recovery must also preserve or conservatively cover outstanding exposure. Timeout or cancellation cannot justify releasing a reservation while the original request can still incur charges, and the operational evidence needed to determine final liability remains unspecified. [C3]
The sovereignty argument is similarly narrower than a guarantee against all post-revocation processing. Its stated premise defines revocation at the authorization-decision ordering point, rather than at eventual transmission, receipt or execution. Database fencing cannot by itself stop an already authorized continuation or recall packets beyond campus control, while recipient retention and compliance require separately established obligations. Thus conditional control over an adapter’s invocation initiation does not establish exactly-once provider execution, post-revocation non-delivery or recipient compliance. The proposed authorization, dispatch and recovery transitions remain unimplemented and unapproved. [C3]
The usefulness calculations expose a separate inferential limitation: aggregate success cannot establish sensitive-task coverage. In the supplied hypothetical cohort, $200q_S+800q_N=800$ is compatible with $q_S=0$ and $q_N=1$, so an aggregate useful-completion rate can coexist with no useful sensitive completions. Assigning sensitive tasks admission priority does not repair that gap. Even an explicit sensitive-task marginal usefulness of $0.80$ would not guarantee realized coverage: the recorded common-shock example has all sensitive tasks succeed with probability $0.80$ and all fail with probability $0.20$. These counterexamples constrain interpretation of the scenario without providing measurements of campus workloads. [C3]
The competing economic interpretations remain conditional for the same reason. A gateway with separately approved external access is cheaper in the recorded case where federation adds no usable service or offsetting benefit but does add positive overhead; this does not establish that peer campuses ordinarily contribute no useful capacity. Conversely, nominal peer GPU capacity does not establish capacity that is authorized, reachable, model-compatible and deadline-feasible for a particular request. Actual campus costs, service capacity, workload performance and net federation benefit remain unknown, so neither general federation superiority nor general federation disadvantage follows. [C3]
The proposed pilot does not close these evidential gaps. Its sampling assumptions and hypothetical planning inputs are not established properties of live campus workloads, and its broader ninety-day protocol and decision thresholds remain incompletely operationalized. A paired comparison would also be limited to tasks authorized for both workflows, rather than automatically representing all requested research access. For reliability and safety, the negative constraints remain important: scheduling weights do not establish unconditional latency guarantees or starvation freedom, and finite fault tests with no observed violations would provide bounded support rather than proof of general safety. [C3]
Taken together, these limitations change the interpretation of the central argument: the record identifies conditions under which controlled routing and budget accounting could be coherent, but does not establish that those conditions are contractually available, institutionally accepted or operationally enforced. The strongest supported comparison is therefore between conditional service designs, not demonstrated campus outcomes. Federation’s additional complexity has not been justified by measured useful completions, while the simpler gateway-plus-approved-access alternative has not been established as sufficient for actual demand. [C3]

Open questions and next steps
The next step is to determine whether federation adds a material, policy-permitted research capability beyond a campus gateway with separately approved external access, rather than treating additional remote capacity as sufficient justification. This requires three distinct advances: institutional agreement on permissible processing, an implementation that satisfies the proposed enforcement assumptions, and a workload study demonstrating useful service within actual budgets. None can substitute for the others: an authorization argument does not establish task quality, and greater throughput does not establish affordability or permission to disclose data. [C4]
Source grounding should precede implementation and procurement decisions. The MindRouter documentation and PEARC’26 paper need to be retrieved, versioned, and recorded with actual access dates so that published gateway capabilities can be distinguished from proposed extensions and independently measured behavior. The project-reported review date of 2026-10-31 is historical context, not evidence of a subsequent inspection; federation, frontier routing, and the proposed financial controls must remain hypothetical unless supported separately. Dated provider prices, charging rules, retention terms, and institutional approvals are also required before using a provider in the comparison. [C4]
The first institutional deliverable should be an approved policy specification covering identity, classification inheritance, destinations, retention, audit, recipient obligations, and cross-campus agreements. It must address derived prompts, outputs, logs, embeddings, and caches, rather than approving only the original input. Security and data owners must also decide whether authorization is effective at the home-campus decision point or whether stronger restrictions on later transmission, receipt, execution, and retention are required; the proposed decision-time semantics permit a previously committed grant to transmit after a later revocation. Public or synthetic fixtures should therefore precede real-data testing until the relevant campuses approve both the processing domains and these in-flight semantics. [C4]
The implementation task is to establish the assumptions on which the conditional design depends, not merely to reproduce its state labels. Independent inspection should examine complete mediation of egress and spending, protocol canonicalization, immutable request manifests, authoritative ordering of policy and ledger changes, crash-safe one-use issuance, sender fencing, replay-record retention, and durable reconciliation. Translation and attachment resolution must occur before the request is frozen, while status queries, restarts, and duplicate acknowledgements must not reconstruct sending authority. These are outstanding implementation obligations: no refinement proof or independent inspection currently establishes that the proposed mechanisms satisfy them. [C4]
Fault testing should target the boundaries between authorization, dispatch, and reconciliation, with independent packet and provider observations supplementing protected decision records. The table specifies discriminating tests for the proposed controls; its outcomes are acceptance criteria, not reported results. Financial recovery deserves particular attention because fresh credentials and replay isolation do not restore liabilities omitted by a stale backup, and a current zero-charge response does not establish finality while an original sender can still transmit. Tests should also measure the service lost to safe denial and unresolved reservations, since preserving policy and budget constraints can reduce availability. [C4]
The empirical comparison should retain all four baselines: campus-local inference only, approved frontier API only, a campus gateway with separately approved external access, and that gateway arrangement extended by a hypothetical federation. Each submitted research objective should be registered as one parent task with its input snapshot, rubric, deadline, classification, provenance requirements, and resource envelope; tools, retries, and agent exchanges are descendants rather than additional successes. Overall coverage should use the offered workload, while model-quality comparisons should use matched, policy-eligible strata and report excluded demand explicitly. Correct refusals belong in the safety record, not in the count of delivered research results. [C4]
The workload needs separate observations for coding, routine analysis, scientific reasoning, multimodal or long-context work, agentic workflows, sensitive processing, and reproducible batches. A useful completion should jointly satisfy authorization, the approved quality rubric, the delivery deadline, and required provenance; recording a model-version label alone is not a reproducibility demonstration. Sensitive-stratum usefulness must be measured directly because aggregate success can conceal complete failure on sensitive tasks, and admission priority does not establish usefulness. The recorded candidate protocols also propose different deadlines, so a single approved set of task-specific acceptance rules must be frozen before enrollment rather than selected after observing results. [C4]
The federation comparison should hold non-federation entitlements constant, including local allocations, approved paid access, policy rules, and parent-task spending limits, while counting the added partner capacity and agreement costs explicitly. Paired executions should prevent answer sharing and cross-arm cache leakage, with isolated capacity slices or matched replay windows to avoid mistaking interference from duplicate study execution for ordinary-service latency. Queue rejection, model incompatibility, budget exhaustion, outages, and missed deadlines should remain outcomes rather than post hoc exclusions. Controlled replay and live-service observations should be reported separately because they support different claims about operational behavior. [C4]
Capacity and fairness measurements must identify usable service opportunities, not merely total GPU inventory. Required observations include memory and context fit, compatible capacity, queue blocking, cold starts, maintenance conditions, peak demand, scheduling quanta, and the interaction between batch and interactive work. WDRR weights should not be interpreted as an unconditional latency or starvation-freedom guarantee; the study needs an approved reference allocation and observation window, together with either a demonstrated scheduling guarantee or an explicit report of its absence. Administrative effort and on-call burden should be measured alongside throughput and availability rather than left outside the service comparison. [C4]
The economic study should replace hypothetical ledger entries with campus evidence and preserve the distinction between sunk, incremental, and fully loaded costs. Local accounts need acquisition and replacement cash flows, depreciation, utilization and idle capacity, power and cooling, storage, network, support, procurement, and operations labor, without charging the same replacement or staff activity twice. API accounts need dated rates and contractual bounds covering workflow amplification, retries, tools, embeddings, cache misses, ancillary charges, commitments, and unresolved liabilities; allocation ceilings must not be presented as actual spending. Sensitivity analysis should vary workload mix, demand, utilization, prices, and federation overhead rather than extrapolate constant completion fractions through saturation. [C4]
A reviewed 90-day pilot requires a decision procedure before enrollment, including a declared primary population, approved rubrics, justified precision, multiplicity handling, fairness and administrative ceilings, and explicit inconclusive outcomes. One proposed strengthening would require the lower endpoint of a prespecified paired confidence interval to exceed $0.10$, rather than accepting a point estimate of at least ten percentage points with an interval that merely excludes zero; this is a candidate decision rule, not established institutional policy. Its sample size and power remain unresolved, and independent-pair calculations cannot be transferred uncritically to correlated live workloads or to a differently weighted block analysis. Safety stops should end the efficacy claim as inconclusive, while zero observed violations in finite fault tests should remain bounded evidence rather than a general safety proof. [C4]
The resulting decision should ask whether federation delivers an approved capability or useful-completion gain sufficient to justify its additional operating and governance burden, subject to separate safety, coverage, service, and administrative gates. If usable service opportunities are unchanged, there is no offsetting benefit, and federation adds positive overhead on the chosen cost basis, the simpler gateway arrangement is cheaper under those stated conditions. The evidence therefore changes federation from an assumed solution to an extension that must earn deployment through a demonstrated, policy-permitted advantage over campus service plus separately approved external access. [C4]
