Maximum Regions Formed by Lines v1
==================================

Project
-------
Interpretation: Agent-generated findings are provisional unless explicitly verified; reviewer agreement is not proof.

Research problem: For each integer n >= 0, determine the maximum number of regions into which n distinct straight lines can divide the Euclidean plane. Lines may be placed arbitrarily. Derive a formula for the maximum, prove that no arrangement can exceed it, and show that an arrangement attaining it exists for every n. Include the n = 0 case and check small values. Do not assume a particular proof method.

Strategies
----------
Incremental region counting with an explicit maximizing arrangement (saved)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Define a region as a connected component of the plane after removing the lines. With no lines, the whole plane is one region, so R(0) = 1.

For the upper bound, insert the lines one at a time. The kth line meets the preceding k − 1 lines in at most k − 1 distinct points. Those points divide the new line into at most k open intervals or rays. Each such piece lies in an existing region. Every existing region is convex: it is the intersection of the open half-planes specifying which side of each existing line its points occupy. Consequently, a region cannot contain two separated pieces of the new line. A region met by a piece is split into exactly two regions, since it is open and convex and has points on both sides of the new line. Thus the kth line adds at most k regions. Summing gives R(n) ≤ 1 + ∑(k = 1 to n) k = 1 + n(n + 1)/2.

For attainment, take the n lines L_k: y = kx + k², for k = 1, …, n. When L_k is inserted, its intersection with L_i, where i < k, has x-coordinate −(i + k). These k − 1 coordinates are distinct. Hence L_k is divided into exactly k pieces by the earlier lines. By the convex-region argument above, these pieces lie in distinct existing regions, each of which is split in two. Therefore L_k adds exactly k regions. This arrangement attains 1 + n(n + 1)/2 for every positive n; the empty arrangement attains it for n = 0. Together with the upper bound, this proves the formula.

Separate the bound from attainment (saved)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Prove a new line crosses at most n-1 distinct old intersection points, then show its pieces split distinct convex regions. Independently verify an explicit family with no parallel pairs or triple concurrence. Include n=0 and n=1. Evaluator-authored plan, not a saved session result.
