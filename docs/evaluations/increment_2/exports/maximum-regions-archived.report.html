If a new line has t distinct intersection points with the previous lines, those points divide it into t+1 intervals, including the case t=0. Each interval belongs to one old region. Different intervals belong to different regions: otherwise convexity would force a segment containing an old intersection point to lie inside an old region. Conversely, every old region meeting the new line contains one of these intervals. Thus exactly t+1 old regions meet the new line. Other regions are unchanged, and deleting additional points from the complement cannot merge old components. Consequently the increase is exactly t+1.
