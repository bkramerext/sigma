## Arms, parity, and limits

## Contamination and integrity exclusions

Exclude and mark invalid any arm with a stock solution or archived session in its prompt/context, evaluator-key access, cross-arm transcript exposure, unmatched tool permissions, changed model/settings, answer-containing source in the wrong phase, unbounded retries, or a missing exact-input audit. Do not repair a contaminated arm by silently editing its transcript; restart from a fresh copy and report the discarded run. Disclose any human intervention, model rate limit, provider failure, or interrupted computation. A completed Python/Sage execution is evidence only for the actual code, inputs, and finite scope. Treat a self-described proof as unverified until a reviewer checks every universal inference independently.

## Blinded scorer form and decision

Two independent reviewers see de-identified exports with arm identifiers randomized; they do not see arm labels or each other's scores. For each arm, record: claim and strongest supported scope; correct/incorrect/unknown against the sealed key; decisive cited artifact and reproducibility; objection uptake; continuity between cycles; useful next move; false proof or missing counterexample; calls, tokens, wall time, and cost. Score each quality item 0–4: correctness, continuity, objection uptake, inspectability, uncertainty calibration, and operator usefulness. Record reviewer disagreement and adjudication separately. A no-go is any false universal proof presented as verified, invented source/execution, silently lost decisive counterexample, or shareable report hiding a material unresolved objection. Proceed only to a larger evaluation if A has no critical integrity failure, beats B on blinded usefulness/continuity on at least two tasks, and the GRAPH evidence-on/withheld contrast shows a specific causal benefit without worse unsupported-claim severity. Otherwise the result is a tie, inconclusive, or a research-interaction defect; it is not evidence of general autonomy.
