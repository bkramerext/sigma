# Why SIGMA?

AI assistants can produce compelling answers quickly. But a convincing answer is not necessarily a reliable research result. Difficult questions often require a succession of partial insights, failed approaches, competing explanations, and careful scrutiny of evidence. Without a durable record, an investigation can lose the distinction between what was proposed, what was tested, and what remains uncertain.

**SIGMA exists to make research cumulative.** A question can be explored through different strategies, revisited across sessions, and informed by earlier findings, failures, objections, and open questions. Progress does not always mean solving the entire problem. Ruling out a promising but mistaken direction, clarifying a disputed assumption, or identifying the next decisive test can also be worthwhile outcomes.

It also aims to make **disagreement productive**. An Investigator develops ideas and a Skeptic challenges the reasoning and evidence behind them. The value is not that two agents produce more discussion. Criticism should change what happens next: strengthen an argument, improve an experiment, narrow a claim, or expose a dead end.

**Evidence must be distinguishable from assertion.** A model's report that a computation ran is not the same as a saved execution record; a successful computation does not by itself establish a general claim; and agreement between agents is not independent verification. SIGMA aims to keep the reasoning and its limitations inspectable, without treating a mathematical proof, an experimental result, and a historical inference as interchangeable forms of evidence.

The same principles should be useful in mathematics, science, historical research, and complex scenario analysis, even though those fields demand different kinds of justification. SIGMA is intended to support that breadth without claiming any single procedure can certify every conclusion.

The central proposition must also withstand scrutiny: **does persistent, multi-agent investigation produce better-supported, more useful research than a simpler AI assistant operating with comparable resources?** That advantage has not been established. Testing it is part of SIGMA's purpose.
