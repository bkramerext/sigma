# Operation Narrow Passage: The 36-Hour Crisis

**Sample investigation | Fictional October 2027 crisis | Real-world geographic and historical foundation**

Operation Narrow Passage asks SIGMA to assess a time-critical, high-consequence maritime rescue and diplomatic crisis near **Second Thomas Shoal (Ayungin Shoal)** in the South China Sea. The scenario deliberately presents conflicting objectives and incomplete information, so multiple strategic approaches must be compared. It does **not** supply a prescribed solution or a roster of evaluator agents.

**Reality boundary:** Second Thomas Shoal, the grounded Philippine naval vessel **BRP Sierra Madre**, Philippine and Chinese maritime disagreements, and U.S.-Philippine mutual defense arrangements are real. **The October 2027 collision, casualties, structural emergencies, diplomatic demands, forecasts, and all later injects are invented for this exercise.** This is a strategic decision-analysis scenario, not an actual mission or operational directive.

## Historical foundation for independent research

The sample is grounded in documented events and institutions that a research model can examine:

- **BRP Sierra Madre and Second Thomas Shoal:** The Philippines maintains an outpost aboard a naval ship deliberately grounded at the shoal in 1999. The Philippines and China dispute maritime access and have presented incompatible claims surrounding the shoal.
- **Real confrontations:** Philippine resupply operations have been challenged, including the June 2024 confrontation in which Philippine personnel were injured. Historical incident footage and national accounts have been disputed.
- **A fragile arrangement:** In July 2024, the Philippines and China announced a provisional understanding on resupply to the Sierra Madre. The Philippines subsequently made clear that it had not accepted Chinese onboard inspection as a condition. The scenario posits an invented deterioration of this arrangement in 2027; it does not assert that the agreement actually failed.
- **Alliance commitments:** The United States has publicly stated that Article IV of the **1951 U.S.–Philippines Mutual Defense Treaty** extends to armed attacks on Philippine forces, public vessels, and aircraft, including coast guard ships, anywhere in the South China Sea. Whether a particular ambiguous collision constitutes an *armed attack*, and what governments should do in response, are distinct judgments. The treaty is not an automatic declaration of war.
- **Legal disagreement:** A 2016 arbitral ruling rejected important Chinese maritime claims in the South China Sea; China rejects the ruling. A crisis investigation must distinguish established legal instruments, each government's public positions, and the uncertain practical consequences of any temporary arrangement.

## T+0: Situation at 06:00, Manila time

### Incident at sea

A Philippine coast guard vessel returning from a resupply mission collides with a Chinese coast guard ship near Second Thomas Shoal.

**Fictional situation report:**

| Field | Report at T+0 | Reliability or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Philippine vessel | Disabled; taking on water | Crew reports; no independent inspection |
| Persons aboard | 23 total: 3 dead, 9 injured, 11 not reported injured | Preliminary muster; subject to confirmation |
| Medical condition | 2 critically injured; one may require surgery within 12 hours | Remote physician's estimate; incomplete examination |
| Vessel safety | Unsafe occupancy possible within 24 hours | Preliminary engineering estimate, not a certainty |
| Chinese vessel | Damage and casualties unknown | No independently corroborated report |
| Incident cause | Both governments accuse the other of dangerous or deliberate conduct | Contested; no conclusive independent finding |
| Alleged gunfire | Short online recording appears to show warning shots before impact | Timing, authenticity and completeness disputed |
| Weather | Severe storm forecast to reach the area in approximately 36 hours | Forecast uncertainty; arrival and severity could change |

The rescue problem is not solved by reaching the disabled ship. Patients must receive care, survivors require safe accommodation, and the operation needs permission, resources, and an acceptable political context. Information is missing precisely when decisions are most urgent.

### A second emergency: BRP Sierra Madre

Fourteen Philippine service members remain aboard the **BRP Sierra Madre**, separate from the 23 aboard the disabled coast guard vessel. They report rising water in part of the ship, difficulty assessing structural stability remotely, and degraded communications. It is unclear whether the ship can remain safely occupied through the forecast storm.

Evacuating the Sierra Madre's crew might protect lives but could have significant longer-term implications for Philippine presence at the shoal. Leaving them in place may create unacceptable exposure if the structural reports are accurate. Both the engineering state and the political consequences require explicit assessment.

### Political and legal crisis

- **Philippines:** The president publicly calls the collision an armed attack and asks Washington for assistance, but insists Philippine authorities retain control over Philippine relief and national decisions. There is strong domestic pressure to avoid concessions that could be interpreted as recognizing Chinese jurisdiction over access.
- **China:** Beijing denies responsibility and publicly warns that direct intervention by U.S. armed forces would be a grave escalation. Chinese officials suggest a humanitarian-access discussion if Philippine relief ships accept inspection, while leaving details and authority unresolved.
- **United States:** The administration is consulting with Manila about assistance and mutual defense obligations. Officials have not independently determined whether the incident meets the treaty's armed-attack threshold. A refusal or delay can damage alliance confidence; an intervention can create direct confrontation and additional exposure.
- **Other regional actors:** Potential mediators and medical assistance providers may be available, but none has made an unconditional commitment at T+0. Their permissions, resources, and acceptable terms are unknown.

**None of these fictional government statements is evidence that war is inevitable.** The escalation risk lies in interactions among public commitments, domestic pressure, unclear evidence, physical proximity, ambiguous intent, and successive reactions.

## Mission and constraints

**Mission:** Support Philippine-led efforts to preserve life, evacuate casualties, address the Sierra Madre's crew safety, and restore a durable means of humanitarian access **without precipitating a regional armed conflict**.

Success cannot be reduced to a single evacuation count. Decisions must reconcile:

1. **Immediate survival:** The critically injured need timely treatment; remaining personnel must be rescued or protected.
2. **Political authority:** The Philippine government makes its own decisions. Partners have separate legal responsibilities, limits, and approval processes.
3. **Escalation:** A rescue or show of support that creates new U.S.–China casualties could worsen the crisis dramatically.
4. **Long-term position:** A temporary access arrangement, inspection, or withdrawal may establish a precedent unacceptable to an affected government even if it solves an immediate problem.
5. **Uncertain cause:** Serious allegations of deliberate violence remain unverified at the exact moment officials are under pressure to react.
6. **Capacity and timing:** Teams, transport, treatment facilities, weather tolerance, communications and agreement among actors cannot be assumed available.
7. **Reversibility:** Some decisions can be modified as evidence arrives; others create political or human consequences that are hard to undo.

The exercise intentionally contains **no predetermined preferred strategy**. SIGMA should originate materially different approaches and expose their conflicting prerequisites, hard constraints, failure modes, and uncertainty. An appealing approach can fail because it cannot meet a medical deadline; another can satisfy the rescue objective yet create a severe political or escalation risk. It must be possible to conclude that no available option satisfies every objective, and to identify the least harmful compromises.

## Why the crisis could escalate to wider conflict

A credible pathway is a sequence of contingent developments, not a single automatic trigger:

1. **Ambiguous confrontation causes fatalities.** The casualty figures generate domestic pressure for a decisive response, while an incomplete video is treated differently by each government.
2. **An alliance commitment becomes salient.** Manila's public armed-attack assertion increases pressure on Washington, while the U.S. government still lacks a verified account. An Article IV discussion does not itself settle the law or mandate war.
3. **An urgent humanitarian action is interpreted through the confrontation.** One government sees emergency assistance or protective presence as proportionate; another interprets it as a challenge to its claims or security.
4. **A further incident changes the stakes.** Injury, collision, miscommunication, or an alleged use of force involving American personnel or vessels could lead to new demands for responses.
5. **Decision makers face shrinking room to compromise.** Public commitments and fear of appearing to abandon an ally or concede jurisdiction can make even reversible practical arrangements politically difficult.

SIGMA should evaluate **whether specific decisions make this pathway more or less plausible**, what evidence could discriminate between outcomes, and which off-ramps remain credible. It should not assert numeric escalation probabilities absent a defensible model and inputs.

## Evidence available to the investigation

At T+0, the information above is the complete **exercise briefing**. It contains:

- **Established historical context**, accessible through the listed public sources.
- **Authoritative fictional incident facts**, such as the preliminary passenger count and issued governmental statements, true *within the simulation* unless changed by a later authoritative inject.
- **Provisional reports**, including vessel integrity, the medical deadline, the alleged warning shots, and the storm forecast.
- **Competing claims**, especially about fault and whether force was deliberately used.
- **Unknowns**, including Chinese ship casualties, exact structural conditions, negotiating limits, rescue capacity, and likely government responses.

Unknowns are research obligations, not invitations to invent specifics. Agents can reason conditionally, request needed facts, and test sensitivity to plausible ranges.

## Research assessment

For each substantive approach, a SIGMA investigation should produce:

- Mission objective coverage and any non-negotiable conditions it violates.
- Time, logistical, legal, diplomatic, medical, and political dependencies that must hold.
- Assumptions labeled as evidenced, provided scenario fact, provisional report, or conjecture.
- Indicators that would change feasibility or escalation judgment.
- Potential counterexamples and adverse outcomes, including failures after a superficially successful rescue.
- A recommendation to continue investigating, revise, defer or reject, supported by auditable findings.

Do not preassign six specialist agents or write six fixed answers into the sample. Determining which expertise and investigative perspectives are needed is part of the research process.

### Minimum requirements of a future implementation

- **Event identity and authority:** Each inject has an ID, effective simulated time, author/source class, payload, and explicit uncertainty. Distinguish authoritative scenario updates from reports and agent speculation.
- **State and provenance:** Keep the initial briefing, every inject, state revisions, affected strategy assessments, supporting evidence and decisions as immutable or auditable records.
- **Selective reevaluation:** Identify which assertions, constraints and strategies actually depend on the changed facts. Rerun appropriate checks rather than produce an unrelated full reanalysis.
- **Replay:** Reconstruct the sequence of injects, observations, evaluations, decisions, and human interventions after the fact.
- **Budget and failure handling:** Bounded runs, truthful interruptions, recovery, no fabricated test results, and no silent conversion of uncertainty into confidence.

### Discriminating tests

1. **Changed deadline:** Narrowing the medical window must change the feasibility of any approach that can no longer meet it, without automatically invalidating unrelated assertions.
2. **Attribution uncertainty:** Conflicting witness accounts without authentic video metadata must not become a verified finding of deliberate gunfire.
3. **Authority constraint:** A proposal dependent on accepting an unauthorized inspection fails that prerequisite even if it scores well on immediate rescue.
4. **Escalation sensitivity:** Two approaches with similar expected rescue outcomes can remain meaningfully different because of the possibility and severity of further confrontation.
5. **Outdated assumption:** An earlier storm-time estimate remains in history but is no longer used as the current forecast after the update.
6. **Lineage and recovery:** The final assessment can be reconstructed from dated injects, actual checks, critiques and revisions; interrupted evaluations never masquerade as complete.
7. **No-op:** Repeating the same already-accounted-for report without a changed authority or confidence level must not manufacture new findings.

## Safety and realism boundary

This is a fictional humanitarian and strategic risk assessment grounded in real, documented political geography. It intentionally does not provide tactical ship movements, interception plans, targeting, weapons employment, military vulnerability exploitation, or methods of evading another force. Strategic conclusions should not be mistaken for actionable operational orders.

**Current capability:** Open the sample as a normal SIGMA investigation and explore its baseline problem. **Future capability:** Time-stepped scenario injects with continuous strategy-fitness evaluation, replay, and after-action analysis.
